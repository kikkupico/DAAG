# Hellenistic Greek architecture and building types (323-31 BC), artist reference

Method note: web sources were mostly Wikipedia summaries. Items under "Inferences" marked [background] come from general scholarly knowledge (Lawrence, Coulton, Vitruvius) that I did NOT verify online in this pass; treat as likely but unsourced.

## Temples, orders, roofing, polychromy, masonry

### Takeaway
Hellenistic temples favour Ionic and Corinthian over Doric; Doric columns have no base; walls are ashlar on stepped platforms with timber roofs under terracotta (or marble) tiles; stone was painted/stuccoed in white, blue and red.

### Cited Findings
- "The Doric Temple was virtually abandoned" in the Hellenistic period; Ionic and Corinthian dominated. Alexandria used Corinthian capitals heavily outside and in (Serapeum, 246-221 BC). — [Wikipedia, Hellenistic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_architecture)
- Didyma (Apollo): Ionic double colonnade of 108 columns, nearly 20 m tall, round an inner court; designed by Daphnis and Paionios at the end of the 4th c., never finished. — same source
- Alexandrian ornament: segmental, broken, hollow and volute pediments (baroque, later influential on Roman and Petra). Four Corinthian capital types identified. — same source
- Olympieion, Athens: Cossutius continued it c. 175 BC for Antiochus IV in Corinthian, double peristyle with a third row on the east and west fronts; columns about 17 m tall, 1.7 m diameter, 20 flutes (completed by Hadrian, Roman). — [WorldHistory/search summary via Wikipedia Decimus Cossutius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimus_Cossutius)
- Doric columns lack bases and sit directly on the stylobate (top step); Ionic and Corinthian have bases. — [Wikipedia, Ancient Greek architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_architecture)
- Temple of Zeus at Nemea, c. 330 BC (late Classical, flag: pre-Hellenistic): 6x12 very slender Doric; interior Corinthian columns carrying a smaller Ionic tier; no exterior sculpture. — [The Archaeologist](https://www.thearchaeologist.org/blog/ancient-greek-sanctuaries-the-temple-of-zeus-at-nemea)
- Hermogenes of Priene (late 3rd-early 2nd c. BC) designed the Ionic temple of Artemis Leukophryene at Magnesia on the Maeander; most important Ionic theorist per Vitruvius. — [Wikipedia, Hermogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermogenes_of_Priene)
- Priene, Temple of Athena Polias by Pytheos, funded by Alexander, dedicated 323 BC (Ionic; borderline date). — [Wikipedia, Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene)
- Construction: stepped crepidoma on layered squared-stone foundations; mudbrick walls with timber reinforcement in earlier phases; Doric 18-20 flutes, Ionic/Corinthian typically 24. — [Wikipedia, Ancient Greek temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_temple)
- Colour: white, blue and red; blue triglyphs alternating with red metopes; columns white or stuccoed, decoration richer. — same source
- Tiles: two-piece system, flat tegulae covered by curved imbrices (pan and cover), in use since c. 700-650 BC; replaced thatch for fire resistance. — [Wikipedia, Roof tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof_tiles)
- Tower of the Winds, Athens (c. 50 BC or earlier, late Hellenistic): octagonal Pentelic marble, about 12 m high, about 8 m across, on three steps; two pedimented porches with Corinthian columns (leafy capitals, no volutes); 24 trapezoidal stone roof slabs under a central keystone carved to look like tiles; eight sundials; interior water clock. — [Wikipedia, Tower of the Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Winds)
- Materials by region: Pentelic marble (Athens), grey-veined Marmara marble (Pergamon), Piraeus limestone for walls. — [Pergamon Altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Altar), [Stoa of Attalos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoa_of_Attalos)

### Inferences
- [background] Hellenistic Doric is still used for stoa outer fronts, gymnasium/palaestra courts and houses (Delos Rhodian peristyles), not for big temples. Hellenistic Doric is lighter, more widely spaced, unfluted lower shafts sometimes, and lacks the Classical swelling. Wall courses are isodomic ashlar; roofs: low-pitched gable with raking sima, painted terracotta antefixes (palmette) along eaves, often marble tiles on prestigious temples. Pitched timber trusses/rafters hidden above a coffered ceiling.
- Practical artist rule: plain white-stuccoed or white-marble shafts; coloured accents on entablature (blue/red), painted dentils and egg-and-dart, bronze or gilded details; Doric = no base, Ionic/Corinthian = moulded base.
- Consistent with the project's costume and roof canon (unbased Doric columns, pan-and-cover tiles).

### Gaps
- No verified proportions for Hellenistic Doric vs Classical; no verified source on antefix forms or marble roof tiles.
- Polychromy of specific Hellenistic buildings is mostly inferred; Pergamon Altar paint "likely, though no traces survive" per Wikipedia.

## Altars, stoas, libraries, bouleuteria, theatres, gymnasia, stadia

### Takeaway
Civic centres are built from long colonnaded stoas, tiered theatres with a high stone stage front, peristyle palaestrae and nearly-square council halls with raked seating; Pergamon and Priene show them best.

### Cited Findings
- Pergamon Altar (Eumenes II, c. 166-156 BC): near-square (about 36 x 33 m) on a high podium with five steps; west stair nearly 20 m wide, about 6 m high; grid of tuff foundation walls; Ionic colonnade round an inner court holding the fire altar; 113 m Gigantomachy frieze outside, Telephos frieze inside; grey-veined Marmara marble, darker Lesbos stone. — [Wikipedia, Pergamon Altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Altar)
- Stoa of Attalos, Athens (159-138 BC): 115 x 20 m, two storeys, Pentelic marble front and limestone walls; ground floor Doric outside / Ionic inside, upper floor Ionic outside / Pergamene inside; 42 shops (21 per floor) at the back; stairs at each end; small windows and doors in rear wall; rebuilt 1952-56. — [Wikipedia, Stoa of Attalos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoa_of_Attalos)
- Pergamon library: four rooms, main reading room small; gap about 50 cm between walls and shelves for air circulation; claimed 200,000 volumes. — [Wikipedia, Library of Pergamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Pergamum)
- Pergamon: agora with stoas, administrative buildings, sanctuaries, colossal theatre for about 10,000. — [Hellenistic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_architecture)
- Theatre of Dionysus Athens: 4th-c. Lycurgan stone phase with 78 rows in 13 wedges and front-row honorary seats (prohedria); Hellenistic phase added a permanent stone proskenion. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Dionysus)
- Priene has bouleuterion, prytaneion, theatre, gymnasium, stadium, agora; Commons lists Upper and Lower Gymnasia, Alexandreion, Sanctuary of Asclepius and Egyptian gods. — [Wikipedia, Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene), [Commons Category:Priene](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Priene)
- Gymnasia: courts enclosed by stoas into a peristyle; palaestra identified by peristyle + exedra + loutron; xystos covered track; Pergamon's Upper Gymnasium (Eumenes II, 2nd c.) palaestra about 36 x 74 m inside stoas; Delphi palaestra (late 4th c.) 32 m square with Ionic peristyle. Priene and Pergamon have stadia. — [search summary, BSA/Wikipedia](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/25044/bsa_058_07.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y), [Palaestra at Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestra_at_Delphi)
- Priene altar of Athena Polias reconstructed with balustrade and Muse relief (Pergamon Museum). — [Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Altar_of_Athena_Polias_at_Priene,_reconstruction_with_original_balustrade_and_relief_with_a_Muse,_2nd_century_BC,_Pergamon_Museum_Berlin_(8405294370).jpg)

### Inferences
- [background] Bouleuteria are roofed squarish halls with steeply raked seats on three or four sides (Priene: wooden roof spanning without interior supports is debated, possible central supports). Hellenistic theatres: tall narrow two-storey stage building with painted panels (pinakes) between half-columns, orchestra circle, steep cavea cut into hill. Pergamon theatre is unusually steep with a terrace in front; stage was dismountable (timber).
- Pergamene order: capital with palm/leaf forms (not verified here).

### Gaps
- Bouleuterion roofing and stage-building details not verified online. Stadium form (long track, one rounded end, stone starting lines, seating on embankments) not sourced.

## Fountain houses, lighthouses, tombs/monuments, towers and walls

### Takeaway
Utility and monument types: lighthouse of Alexandria (three tapering tiers), fortified masonry walls with multi-storey artillery towers, and honorific marble monuments; fountain houses and tombs are thinly documented here.

### Cited Findings
- Pharos of Alexandria, 284-246 BC: three tiers (square, octagonal, circular), limestone and granite, about 103-118 m, statues (Tritons at corners, Zeus or Poseidon on top), architect (Sostratus of Cnidus) disputed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria)
- Colossus of Rhodes about 32 m; Pergamon altar and Didyma as monumental. — [Hellenistic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_architecture)
- Towers: earlier single-storey squares; Hellenistic towers are multi-storey for catapults, increasingly detached from walls; ashlar of small rectangular blocks. — [Hellenistic fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_fortifications)
- Aigosthena: isodomic ashlar (hammered faces, drafted corners), pseudo-isodomic and near-polygonal masonry; eight artillery towers; very well preserved. — [Wikipedia, Aigosthena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aigosthena)
- Priene: grid plan with six main streets (about 6 m) and fifteen narrow cross streets (about 3 m); west gate and walls on Commons. — [Wikipedia, Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene)

### Inferences
- [background] Fountain houses: rectangular, Doric or Ionic columned front screening basins, roofed with tiles (Peirene, Corinth; Hellenistic remodel). Tombs: Macedonian vaulted chamber tombs with painted Doric/Ionic facades (Vergina, Lefkadia); Mausoleum at Halicarnassus (c. 350 BC, pre-Hellenistic) as model; monumental pillar monuments (Delphi, Paullus pillar 168 BC); round tombs. Walls: stone socle with mudbrick upper courses in rural contexts; stone-courses with parapet and crenellations. All unverified.

### Gaps
- No sourced details on fountain houses or tomb forms; Rhodes (Vitruvius's theatre-like harbour) not covered.

## Private houses (Delos, Priene), furniture, daily life

### Takeaway
Houses face inward on a courtyard; Priene has the prostas type, Delos has elaborate peristyle houses with cisterns and mosaics; floors in pebble or tessera mosaic, walls in painted stucco.

### Cited Findings
- Priene houses (Wiegand and Schrader): standard rectangular insulae; prostas house with prostas/pastas porch, oikos, andron and adjoining room facing north onto a courtyard; first peristyle houses appear in the Hellenistic period. — [Gonul / METU thesis](https://etd.lib.metu.edu.tr/upload/12623056/index.pdf) and search summary
- About one third of Priene houses had indoor toilets; water via aqueducts and cisterns. — [Wikipedia, Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene)
- Delos House of the Trident (c. 150-100 BC): two storeys, Rhodian peristyle (Doric columns), impluvium over a rainwater cistern, dolphin and anchor mosaic in propylon, trident mosaic in peristyle. — [Perseus](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Delos%2C+House+of+the+Trident&object=Building)
- Delos House of the Dolphins: opus tessellatum mosaic with rosette, Cupids on dolphins, signed by Asklepiades of Arados, late 2nd c. BC. — [Mosaic Projects](https://mosaic-projects.com/2022/10/13/delos-dolphin-the-research-behind-it/)
- Delos became a free port 166 BC, slave market, sacked by Mithridates 88 BC. — [Wikipedia, Delos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delos)
- Courtyards were most often paved with pebbles; mosaic courtyards occur; Olynthus houses (4th c., flag pre-Hellenistic) of stone, wood and mudbrick; mosaic techniques include tesserae and opus vermiculatum. — [Hellenistic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_architecture)

### Inferences
- [background] Walls: rubble or ashlar socle with mudbrick above, plastered and painted in the Masonry Style (stucco blocks in red, yellow, white, with relief joints), first appearing in Hellenistic Delos and Priene. Roofs: low pitched, terracotta pan-and-cover, courtyard impluvium opening; rooms with raised bench rims (andron with off-centre door for couches); timber doors with bronze studs; small high windows; upper floors via timber stair; lamps are moulded terracotta and bronze. Not verified; Masonry Style page returned 404.

### Gaps
- No sourced details on furniture, doors, windows, lamps, Masonry Style colours; rural farmhouses/towers (e.g., Attic and island tower houses) and shrines not found; harbour shipsheds and warehouses only generically (Delos commercial harbour with five basins, Agora of the Competaliasts, Stoa of Antigonos two-aisled) — see [Peter Sommer](https://www.petersommer.com/blog/greece-travel/delos-island-greece).
