One problem, three settings

Many machines, acting as one

Every book in the series asks how a group of machines, each with its own memory and joined only by messages, can behave as a single trustworthy one. These three books take the question in order.

Arche finds an order of events that machines can agree on without a shared clock. Paxos keeps one log of commands in such an order while machines stop, restart and lose messages. Schedia keeps the same log with one machine in charge at a time, and shows how another takes over when it falls silent without losing anything the last one promised.

Arche · the ring road

Ordering Without Clocks

Three trading houses sit round a mountain, and news passes between them only on slips carried along one ring road, where no messenger passes another and no journey has a known length. Clouds hide the sun, so no house can read the hour.

After Leslie Lamport, Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System, 1978 · Colin J. Fidge and Friedemann Mattern, 1988

Paxos · the Chamber

The Part-Time Parliament

The legislators of Paxos wander in and out of a round Chamber as their trade allows, and speak only through messengers who may be slow, lose a message, or deliver it twice. Each keeps the law in a scroll of their own, in ink that never changes.

After Leslie Lamport, The Part-Time Parliament, 1998

Schedia · the Tholos

One Leader at a Time

Schedia, a city on the eastern arm of Paxos, makes its law in the Tholos, a round hall where five legislators keep copies of the law on wax tablets. One of them at a time holds the podium and alone adds lines, and going home smooths the wax.

After Brian M. Oki and Barbara H. Liskov, Viewstamped Replication, 1988
