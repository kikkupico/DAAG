# Measured dimensions of Hellenistic Greek arms, armour, siege engines, clothing, bodies (for 3D modelling)

Coverage note: research was cut short at roughly 24 tool calls, mostly secondary (Wikipedia, search summaries). Only values with a link are in the Cited Findings. Everything else is in Gaps. Wikipedia's pages gave no figures for many items, so large parts of the brief are unanswered. Treat all "certainty" labels as: P = primary/museum record, S = secondary summary, W = Wikipedia-level.

## Infantry and cavalry weapons (sarissa, doru, swords, javelins, bows, slings)

### Takeaway
Sarissa and sword figures are available only at Wikipedia-summary level. The only archaeological sarissa-head figure found is a 51 cm Vergina head. Doru, akontion, thureophoros spear, machaira, dagger, xyston and Cretan bow figures were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Infantry sarissa: about 5 to 7 m overall. Certainty W, Hellenistic (Macedonian, 4th-2nd c. BC) — [Wikipedia: Sarissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa)
- Polybius: 16 cubits in the original design, reduced in practice to 14 (about 6.3 m by the page's conversion). Theophrastus: longest sarissa 12 cubits (about 5.8 m). Literary, Hellenistic (Polybius 2nd c. BC) — [Wikipedia: Sarissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa)
- Cavalry sarissa: about 4.5 m; Peter Connolly tested up to 4.9 m. Modern reconstruction. — [Wikipedia: Sarissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa)
- Sarissa weight: traditional estimate about 5.5 to 6.5 kg; Connolly cherry-wood reconstruction 5.8 m long, 4.05 kg. Modern estimate, not measured ancient. — [Wikipedia: Sarissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa)
- Sarissa head: Vergina find 51 cm leaf-shaped (Hellenistic/Late Classical context, certainty W); Sekunda's estimate about 13.5 cm diamond-shaped iron head (estimate). The two conflict by a factor of nearly 4, so choose one and label it. — [Wikipedia: Sarissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa)
- Sarissa shaft: ash or cornel wood, probably slightly tapered. No diameter and no sauroter (butt-spike) length given. — [Wikipedia: Sarissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa)
- Xiphos: blade about 45-60 cm, Spartans supposedly as short as 30 cm; weight about 450-900 g. Mostly Archaic/Classical data, not Hellenistic-specific. — [Wikipedia: Xiphos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiphos)
- Kopis: early examples blade up to 65 cm (Archaic/Classical); later Macedonian examples about 48 cm (Hellenistic-relevant). No weight given. — [Wikipedia: Kopis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopis)
- Lead sling bullet (glans), almond type: typically 35 mm long, 20 mm wide, 28 g; sling length about 61-100 cm; range over 400 m. Not period-specific (spans Classical to Roman); lead bullets from Athens, 4th c. BC, in the British Museum. — [Wikipedia: Sling bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sling_bullet)

### Inferences
- A 5.8-6.3 m sarissa at about 4-6.5 kg is the best-supported infantry model. Cavalry xyston/sarissa about 4.5 m rests on a single reconstruction.
- The 51 cm Vergina head is real archaeological evidence. The 13.5 cm head is a scholarly estimate. Pick one for modelling and label it.

### Gaps
- Sarissa shaft diameter, sauroter length, doru length/weight, akontion, thureophoros spear, machaira and dagger blade data, xyston, Cretan bow and arrow sizes: not found in the sources fetched. Not given from memory.
- Museum-record values (Met/British Museum/Walters) for swords and glandes were not retrieved.

## Shields, helmets, body armour

### Takeaway
Aspis size is cited; Boeotian-type helmet height is one Met record (labelled Roman, mid-2nd c. BC); one pilos mass is from an MFA Boston snippet. Thureos, pelte and linothorax numbers were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Aspis (hoplon): diameter often exceeds 0.9 m; about 7 kg; about 2.5-4 cm thick, deeply dished. Archaic/Classical data; Hellenistic use was reduced. — [Wikipedia: Aspis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspis)
- Thureos: large oval, sometimes nearly rectangular; wood covered with leather, spined boss, central handgrip. No numeric size on the page. — [Wikipedia: Thureos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thureos)
- Helmet of Boeotian type with belt, bronze and lead: H. 22 cm (helmet), belt diameter 33 cm; dated mid-2nd c. BC; catalogued as Roman culture (flag: Roman-period, Hellenistic-type form). Certainty P (Met record) — [Met Open Access object 777855](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/777855)
- Pilos-type helmet, MFA Boston, c. 350-300 BC (Late Classical/Early Hellenistic): mass 603 g. Reported via a search snippet, not checked on the record page — [MFA Boston](https://collections.mfa.org/objects/459289/helmet-of-the-pilos-type)
- Linothorax: very little known about how it was made; Connolly's glued-layer reconstruction (1970s) rests on a misremembered summary of a Byzantine chronicle, not an ancient text; Granger-Taylor proposes twining. No thickness/weight given — [Wikipedia: Linothorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linothorax)

### Inferences
- The 22 cm Boeotian height is a useful upper-body proportion check (skull about 22 cm high with brim) but is one specimen.

### Gaps
- Pelte diameter, thureos height/width, hexagonal/oval specifics, peltast shield, Phrygian/Thracian/Attic/Chalcidian helmet heights and masses, muscle cuirass weight/thickness, greaves, belts, cataphract gear: not found.

## Siege and artillery

### Takeaway
Philo's and Vitruvius's formulas are cited; the Wikipedia table figures looked internally inconsistent, so recompute from the formulas rather than copy the table. Helepolis literary numbers differ slightly between Vitruvius, Diodorus-derived summaries and Wikipedia.

### Cited Findings
- Stone-thrower spring-hole diameter: d = 1.1 x cube root of (100 x m), d in dactyls (1 dactyl = 1.93 cm), m = stone mass in Attic minae (1 mina = about 437 g). Philo (c. 270 BC tradition); Vitruvius also gives it — [Wikipedia: Torsion siege engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_siege_engine); [Wikipedia: Greek and Roman artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_and_Roman_artillery)
- Bolt-thrower: hole diameter = bolt length / 9; other parts are fixed multiples of the hole diameter (for example arm length 7 diameters) — [Wikipedia: Torsion siege engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_siege_engine); search summary of Philo via [Schiefsky, Belopoeica of Philo](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/schiefsky/files/philo_final_proofs_2015-01-29.pdf) (PDF not opened)
- Wikipedia's table rows: hand-held arrow-firer 31 cm bolt, 3.4 cm spring; medium ballista 54 cm bolt, 5.6 cm spring, 1.4 m long; large stone-thrower 13.6 cm spring, 3.4 m long. The same table's "123 cm stones" and "2-talent (26 kg)" labels are inconsistent (26 kg is 1 talent), so flag as suspect — [Wikipedia: Torsion siege engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_siege_engine)
- 1 talent stone (about 26 kg) typical; Archimedes' heaviest up to 3 talents (78 kg); range over 460 m maximum, effective much shorter (about 150 m); most Hellenistic projectiles under 15 kg — [Wikipedia: Ballista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballista); [Wikipedia: Torsion siege engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_siege_engine)
- Gastraphetes: no archaeological finds or pictures; reconstruction relies on Heron's Belopoeica (drawing on Ctesibius); oxybeles is the larger siege version. No sizes given — [Wikipedia: Gastraphetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastraphetes)
- Helepolis (Demetrius at Rhodes, 305-304 BC, built by Epimachus): Wikipedia gives 130 ft (40 m) high, 65 ft (20 m) wide, 8 wheels each 15 ft (5 m) high, 160 short tons, 3,400 men; at least 7 floors with two stairways; iron plates on three sides — [Wikipedia: Helepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helepolis)
- Another summary: about 140 ft (90 cubits, 43 m), nine storeys, top storey 900 sq ft; Vitruvius (Morgan translation) 135 ft high and 60 ft broad — [WarHistory.org](https://weaponsandwarfare.com/2015/12/24/rhodes-305-304-bc), summary relayed by search; primary text at [Vitruvius X, LacusCurtius](http://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/e/roman/texts/vitruvius/10*.html) (not read in full)

### Inferences
- Worked example from the formula: a 1-talent (60 mina, about 26 kg) stone gives d = 1.1 x cube root(6000) = about 20 dactyls = about 39 cm spring hole. Computed here, not read from a source.
- Helepolis dimensions are literary, with a roughly 130-140 ft spread between authors; model as about 40-43 m tall, about 20 m base.

### Gaps
- Euthytonon/palintonon reconstructed sizes (Marsden, Kotsanas), Philo's wall and tower thickness, battering ram, tortoise, ramps and siege towers other than helepolis: not found.

## Human proportions and animals

### Takeaway
Skeletal means are about 171 cm for Hellenistic men and about 153 cm for women, from secondary web summaries only.

### Cited Findings
- Classical Greek male mean 170.5 cm; Hellenistic male mean 171.9 cm (28 individuals); female about 153 cm; ranges over 10,000 years: men 159.7-170.6 cm, women 153.1-160.4 cm. Attributed to J. L. Angel's skeletal studies — [search summary via Facts and Details](https://europe.factsanddetails.com/article/entry-215.html) (low certainty: aggregator, primary Angel not opened)
- War elephants about 3,500-5,000 kg; African forest elephants smaller and harder to tame — [Wikipedia: War elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_elephant) (no shoulder heights given)

### Inferences
- The brief's 1.65-1.70 m men figure is lower than the Angel-derived 1.70-1.72 m; the difference is source choice.

### Gaps
- Lysippan canon (8 heads), child heights, horse/mule/donkey/ox/dog sizes, elephant shoulder heights: not found.

## Clothing and personal items

### Takeaway
Only himation and chlamys dimensions came back, from a popular-reference search summary. None of it is museum-measured.

### Cited Findings
- Himation: rectangular wool, about 4-5 m long by 1.2-1.5 m wide (another source: 5 yards x 1 yard, about 4.6 x 0.9 m). Low certainty, reenactor/popular sources — [search results incl. World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/20/ancient-greek-clothing/)
- Chlamys: about 6-7 ft x 3.5 ft (about 1.8-2.1 m x 1.1 m), nearly square when doubled. Low certainty, same source set — [fashion-era.com](https://fashion-era.com/ancient-costume/chiton-clothing)
- Wikipedia's clothing article gives no measurements for chiton, peplos, exomis, petasos, fibula, sandals — [Wikipedia: Clothing in ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_ancient_Greece)

### Inferences
- The himation range in the brief (2-3 m) conflicts with the 4-5 m found; no sourced Hellenistic measurement resolves it.

### Gaps
- Chiton/peplos cloth size, petasos brim and crown, kausia, pilos hat, sandal sole length, fibula length, pera, staff, kerykeion, hem weights, wreaths, jewellery sizes, Tanagra fans, loom widths and textile weights: not found.
- Next step if continued: Met object API records (sandals, fibulae, jewellery, Tanagra figurines), Marsden vol. 1/2 for artillery tables, Angel's 1984 stature paper, Vitruvius X.16 for helepolis and tortoise.
