# Measured dimensions of Hellenistic Greek tools, objects, ships, vehicles (for 3D modelling)

(Work in progress; appended in batches. Certainty: H = museum/excavation figure, M = encyclopedic summary of scholarship, E = estimate.)

## Ships, Diolkos, Antikythera, Tower of the Winds

### Takeaway
Kyrenia ship (c. 294-291 BC) is the best Hellenistic hull: ~14 m long, 4.4 m beam. Trireme shipshed and Diolkos gauge give hard numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Kyrenia ship (Hellenistic, sank 294-291 BC): preserved hull almost 14.7 x 4.4 m; table gives length 14 m, beam 4.4 m, single square sail, 4-5 kn, crew 4; carried 381 amphorae (Rhodes 340, Knidos 4, Kos 2, Samos 1 ...). Certainty M. — [Wikipedia Kyrenia ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_ship)
- Trireme (Classical, 5th-4th c. BC; used in Hellenistic navies as a type): length just under 37 m (2 cubits per rower, Doric cubit 0.49 m); Piraeus sheds ~40 m long, 6 m wide; draught ~1 m; hull height above water ~2.15 m; ~200 crew, 170 rowers (62 thranitai, 54 zygitai, 54 thalamitai). Olympias replica: 9 kn max, 4 kn steady. Certainty M. Classical, not Hellenistic. — [Wikipedia Trireme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trireme)
- Diolkos (Archaic origin c. 600 BC, operating to 1st c. AD, so used in Hellenistic period): length 6-8.5 km; roadway 3.4-6 m wide; parallel grooves ~1.60 m apart (average gauge ~160 cm); hard limestone paving; max elevation ~79 m; average gradient 1:70, steepest 1:16.5; ~1,100 m archaeologically traced. Certainty M. — [Wikipedia Diolkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diolkos)
- Antikythera mechanism (c. 2nd-1st c. BC): wooden-framed case 34 x 18 x 9 cm; largest gear ~13 cm diameter, 223 teeth; tooth circular pitch avg 1.6 mm, wheel thickness 1.4 mm, air gap 1.2 mm; ~30-35 gears; fragments A 180x150 mm (369 g), B 125x60 (99 g), C 120x110 (64 g), D 45x35 (15 g), E 60x35 (22 g), F 90x80 (86 g), G 125x110 (32 g); 82 fragments. Case size is a reconstruction. Certainty M. — [Wikipedia Antikythera mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism)
- Tower of the Winds, Athens (1st c. BC, by c. 50 BC): octagonal, ~12 m tall, ~8 m diameter; water clock fed from Clepsydra spring on the Acropolis, rear turret as tank, mechanism lost; reconstructions uncertain. Certainty M. — [Wikipedia Tower of the Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Winds)

### Gaps (so far)
- Kyrenia depth, draft, mast, sail area, plank thickness; Kyrenia II figures (not on Wikipedia page).

## Kyrenia II, Zea shipsheds, amphorae, vessels, coins, kleroterion (batch 2)

Note: Met Museum public API returned Incapsula bot-block/403/410 from this environment, so Met figures below come from search-engine snippets of the Met object pages (cited per page); each should be spot-checked on the page.

### Cited Findings
- Kyrenia II (1985 replica): 14.3 m long, 4.4 m beam, mostly Samos Aleppo pine; sail 64 m2 ("educated guess" as to original); original keel 9.33 m, single Aleppo-pine log; mortise-and-tenon planking. Mast height, plank thickness, depth not found. Certainty M-low (search summary). — [Kyrenia ship project](http://www.kyreniaship.agrino.org/KyreniaShipProject.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_ship); [Hull interim report](https://www.academia.edu/12489864/1985_The_Kyrenia_Ship_An_Interim_Report_on_its_Hull_Construction)
- Zea (Piraeus) shipsheds (4th c. BC, Classical; used into Hellenistic): shed width 3.90-4.40 m; interaxial 6.5 m between sheds; double shed length ~80 m (Phase 3 reconstructed 80.02 m); shoreline ~955 m. Certainty M. Classical. — [Zea Harbour Project / Lovén](https://www.academia.edu/41862912/The_Ancient_Harbours_of_the_Piraeus_Volume_I_1_The_Zea_Shipsheds_and_Slipways_Architecture_and_Topography); [Gabrielsen 2014](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/GreeceContinental/Piraeus-Gabrielsen2014.pdf)
- Rhodian amphora (Hellenistic): height ~96-97 cm (a Bibalex/ADS record), width 23-24 cm, rim 14-15 cm; capacity ~20-25 L average, 27.3 L c. 230-200 BC falling to 26.2 L c. 200 BC and 25.4 L c. 187 BC. Study heights 0.765-0.792 m for some examples. Full weight not sourced (estimate in Inferences). Certainty M. — [Hesperia, Rhodian capacities](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/147952.pdf); [Monachov, Rhodian amphoras](https://antikmuseet.au.dk/fileadmin/www.antikmuseet.au.dk/Pontosfiler/BSS_3/BSS3_04_monachov.pdf); [ADS amphora archive](https://archaeologydataservice.ac.uk/archives/view/amphora_ahrb_2005/character.cfm?id=74)
- Koan amphora: ~44 L average (nearly double Rhodian/Knidian). Knidian: ~40 L early 3rd c. BC falling to ~31 L late 2nd c. BC (slimmer); whole vessels match 11-12 Attic choes (36.1-39.4 L) and last two ~30 L = 9 choes (29.55 L); small Knidian amphorae 0.297-0.35 m high, 2.8-3.3 L (grave gifts). Certainty M. Chian amphora: not found. — [Tracing networks (Rhodian/Knidian/Koan)](https://www.academia.edu/44921166/Tracing_Networks_of_the_Hellenistic_Amphora_Market_A_Study_Based_on_Rhodian_Knidian_and_Koan_Transport_Amphoras_2021_); [Small Knidian types](https://www.academia.edu/41249462/THE_SMALL_KNIDIAN_AMPHORA_TYPES); [Knidian typology](http://projects.chass.utoronto.ca/amphoras/trans/sym-t99.htm)
- Megarian (mould-made) bowls, Hellenistic, Met: Argive c. 165-150 BC h 6.2 cm, d 11.1 cm; Asia Minor 2nd-mid 1st c. BC h 6.5, d 11.8 cm; probably Boeotian h 7.8 cm; Boeotian 2nd c. BC h 8.3 cm, w 14 cm. Certainty H (snippet). — [Met 246744](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/246744); [Met 252677](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/252677); [Met 253341](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/253341); [Met 253340](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/253340)
- Hadra hydriai (Ptolemaic Alexandria, Hellenistic, funerary ash urns): h 42.2 x d 26.7 cm (Cretan, 226-225 BC); h 40.5 x 27.8 cm (3rd c.); h 37.8 x 25 cm; h 36.2 x 23.5 cm; 46.4 x 30.2 cm (c. 213 BC). Apulian hydria c. 330-300 BC h 72.7 cm (Late Classical/early Hellenistic, large). Certainty H (snippet). — [Met 245543](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/245543); [Met 245582](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/245582); [Met 245576](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/245576); [Met 245577](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/245577); [Met 245549](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/245549); [Met 248184](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/248184)
- Mould-made lamps, Hellenistic: Syria-Palestine, 2nd c. BC, L 6.7 cm; Greek or Roman, 1st c. BC-1st c. AD (borderline), H 3 cm, L 8.4 cm; wheel-made Greek lamp c. 350-250 BC, 5.7 x 11.7 cm. Certainty H (snippet). — [Met 244870](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/244870); [Met 241528](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/241528); [Met 241700](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/241700)
- Terracotta banquet group with kline (3rd-2nd c. BC), Met 2016.253: 22.9 cm (model, not life size; use for proportions only). — [Met 711029](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/711029)
- Coins: Attic-standard tetradrachm ~17.2-17.3 g; MFA example 27 mm, 17.07 g (Ptolemy I, Alexander-head type). Ptolemaic (Phoenician) tetradrachm ~14.2-14.3 g, so smaller. Certainty M. — [MFA 1861](https://collections.mfa.org/objects/1861); [Wikipedia Attic weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attic_weight); [Wikipedia Ptolemaic coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_coinage); [Kelsey blog](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/kelsey-coin-blog/2020/11/11/hellenistic-currency-systems-in-the-kelsey-the-ptolemies-part-i/)

Classical/Hellenistic. — [Odysseus, Ministry of Culture](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/4/eh430.jsp?obj_id=10323); [Equality by Lot](https://equalitybylot.com/2025/02/08/the-french-school-of-athens-builds-a-kleroterion/)

## Furniture, lifting (batch 3)

### Cited Findings
- Kline: typical ancient Greek klinai ~2-3 m long, ~1 m wide, 70-90 cm high (generic summary from a low-grade source, no named specimen; certainty low). Few wooden klinai survive; evidence from vases, sculpture, tomb stone/bronze replicas. Date range archaic to Hellenistic, not specific. — [Study.com summary](https://study.com/academy/lesson/greek-furniture-history-style.html); [Wikipedia Ancient furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_furniture). Macedonian-tomb stone klinai (Vergina, Kasta) exist but dimensions not retrieved: [Kasta Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasta_Tomb).
- Cranes: Vitruvius De Arch. 10.2 and Heron Mechanica 3.2-5 describe trispastos (3:1) and pentaspastos (5:1) pulley sets and polyspastos; polyspastos with winch/4 men each side lifts ~3000 kg, with treadwheel ~6000 kg (modern popular summaries of the Roman crane, Roman-era, not Hellenistic measured). Certainty low-M. — [Wikipedia Treadwheel crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treadwheel_crane); [UNRV](https://www.unrv.com/articles/how-roman-cranes-lifted-heavy-stone-blocks.php)
- Lewis: iron lewis and dovetail lewis holes appear at Pergamon c. 220 BC; Hellenistic lewis holes (e.g. Didyma drums) smaller, narrower and neater than Roman; average lewis rated 4-6 t; T-shaped/cross lewis holes c. 16-20 x 6-8 x 19-24 cm (this last figure is for Roman examples per the Grokipedia snippet; verify). — [Lewises at Pergamon](https://www.academia.edu/40107931/Lewises_in_Hellenistic_and_Roman_Building_at_Pergamon); [Coulton, Lifting in Early Greek Architecture](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/BAAG112/630416_Lifting%20in%20Early%20Greek%20Architecture_Coulton_Journal%20of%20Hellenic%20Studies1974.pdf); [Grokipedia Lewis](https://grokipedia.com/page/Lewis_(lifting_appliance))

## Papyrus, loom, mills, animals, civic (batch 4)

### Cited Findings
- Papyrus roll (Greco-Roman incl. Hellenistic): height typically 19-24 cm (Herculaneum average ~22 cm; one example 21.2 cm), rolled diameter 4-6 cm; standard roll = 20 sheets pasted (~5 m, Pliny), up to ~10 m; text columns 7.5-9 cm wide (one example 16.7 x 9 cm). Herculaneum/Pliny evidence is Roman-era; Hellenistic norm assumed similar. Certainty M. — [UCL Digital Egypt, Papyrus size](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt//craft/papyrussize.html); [Dartmouth, ancient scroll](https://sites.dartmouth.edu/ancientbooks/2016/05/24/the-historical-background-of-the-ancient-scroll/); [Roll and Codex](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/roll-and-codex)
- Loom weights (Hellenistic): truncated-pyramid clay weights averaging 153.45 g; at Trypitos, Crete, pyramidal weights mostly 50-55 g; Kalydon weights 50-280 g, mostly conical. Warp-weighted loom was the main Greek loom. Certainty M. — [Trypitos loomweights](https://www.academia.edu/8220515/THE_LOOMWEIGHTS_OF_THE_HELLENISTIC_SETTLEMENT_AT_TRYPITOS_SITEIA); [Kalydon loom weights](https://www.academia.edu/105903948/Loom_Weights_from_Kalydon_2023_); [Wikipedia Warp-weighted loom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp-weighted_loom)
- Olive trapetum: Hellenistic rotary crusher (two curved stones turned by a rectangular beam); examples at Priene and Delos (poorly dated); no dimensions retrieved. Olynthus hopper mill: types exist, no dimensions retrieved. — [Mill survey](https://scispace.com/pdf/a-survey-of-ancient-grain-milling-systems-in-the-45d9aux9pe.pdf); [Villa Vigla](https://www.villavigla.com/blog/lithari-the-olive-mill-through-the-ages.html)
- Equids: Cretan horse withers 130-147 cm (avg 140 male, 134 female); Late Mycenaean Chania horses 111-131 cm; Lefkandi horses c. 950 BC ~114 cm (Bronze/Iron Age, not Hellenistic); Greek donkey ~200 kg, rarely above 125 cm at withers (modern breed, indicative). Hellenistic Pherae study exists but gave no withers figures in the snippet. Certainty M-low. — [Klontza-Jaklova, Cheiron 2023](https://trivent-publishing.eu/img/cms/7-%20Cheiron_2-2023_V%C4%9Bra%20Klontza-Jaklov%C3%A1.pdf); [Greek donkey, Amalthia](https://www.amalthia.org/en/component/content/article?id=163%3Agreek-donkey); [Pherae](https://doi.org/10.1080/00934690.2022.2163782)
- Dikast pinakion (bronze juror ticket, 4th c. BC Classical/Late Classical): ~10.8-11.7 cm long, 2.1-2.2 cm wide, 0.2-0.34 cm thick, ~40 g. Bronze psephoi: two discs of identical size, one hollow-axle (pierced), one solid; diameter not retrieved. Certainty M. Classical. — [Spurlock Museum](https://www.spurlock.illinois.edu/collections/search-collection/details.php?a=1930.01.0001); [Met 247975](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/247975); [Wikipedia Pinakion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinakion); [Wikipedia Psephoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psephoi)

## Anchors, hull details, vehicles, coins, stelai, timekeeping, tools (batch 5)

### Cited Findings
- Kyrenia hull as excavated: preserved hull outline 4.8 x 11.4 m (Penn Expedition; differs from Wikipedia's 14.7 x 4.4 m reconstructed/preserved figure; the former is the surviving timber outline, probably a different measure) and ~50% of original wood survived; 403 amphorae recovered over two seasons (Wikipedia says 381 carried; count differs by source). Frame cross-sections ~8.5 cm (Pomey summary of Steffy); 4 cm planking figure was only a guess in my query, NOT confirmed. — [Penn Expedition](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/kyrenia/); [Pomey, Naval architecture](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0167/ch3.xhtml); [Steffy 1985 interim report](https://scispace.com/papers/the-kyrenia-ship-an-interim-report-on-its-hull-construction-2frsxq05ao)
- Kyrenia anchor (c. 300 BC, Type IIb, one-armed wooden anchor with lead-filled stock, Laurion lead): full-size reconstruction weighs 67.4 kg; four lead inserts in stock. Wider ranges: stone anchors 20 kg to >1 t, lead stocks 10 kg-2 t, stone stocks 9-280 kg; Alexandrian coast stone anchors 7-30 kg. Certainty M. — [Anchoring the Kyrenia Ship 2017](https://www.academia.edu/34922554/_2017_Anchoring_the_Kyrenia_Ship_An_experimental_project_to_reconstruct_the_ships_anchor); [Kyrenia anchor, IJNA 2012](https://www.academia.edu/1862054/_2012_The_Anchor_of_the_3rd_Century_BC_Ship_from_Kyrenia_Cyprus_A_one_armed_wooden_anchor_with_a_lead_filled_stock_International_Journal_of_Nautical_Archaeology_41_2_397_407); [Knott 2003, anchors](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/ETUDESarchivees/Navires/Anchors/Anchors-Knott2003.pdf)
- Carts: solid-wheel wagons up to ~1 m wheel diameter, spoked wheels ~1 m (generic popular summary, low certainty). Diolkos gauge ~1.60 m (see above) is the best Greek-specific gauge; the "4 ft 8.5 in" (1.435 m) figure is for Roman roads/Pompeii and a popular-myth source, not Hellenistic Greek evidence. — [Facts and Details, Greek transport](https://europe.factsanddetails.com/article/entry-719.html); [Straight Dope](https://www.straightdope.com/21343664/was-standard-railroad-gauge-4-8-determined-by-roman-chariot-ruts)
- Attic-standard coins: tetradrachm ~17.15-17.5 g, 24-25 mm (Classical Athenian owl, 440-420 BC: 17.15 g, 24 mm); drachma ~4.3 g; obol ~0.72-0.8 g; hemiobol (Macedon, Archaic) 0.3 g. Hellenistic Alexander-type tetradrachms ~27 mm / 17.07 g (MFA above); Ptolemaic ~14.3 g. Diameters of drachma/obol not retrieved. — [Alpha Politismos](https://www.alphapolitismos.gr/en/numismatic-collection/our-coins-and-banknotes/silver-tetradrachm-athens/); [NAVic](https://www.navic.org.au/numismatic-items/the-athenian-tetradrachm/); [Doug Smith, tiny fractionals](https://www.forumancientcoins.com/dougsmith/tiny.html)
- Hellenistic Athenian Agora stelai (Hesperia): complete stele h 1.268 m, w 0.457 m at base (0.428 below moulding), th 0.126 m; ephebic stele h 0.869 m, w 0.47 m, th 0.125 m, letters 5-6 mm; upper-right fragment of white marble h 0.175, w 0.30, th 0.165 m; small fragments h 0.10-0.185 m, th 0.018-0.09 m. Which of the Hesperia articles is which not verified; certainty H for ranges, spot-check. — [Hesperia, inscriptions plates 66-67](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/147933.pdf); [Ephebic inscription](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/147457.pdf); [Decree honoring foreigners](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/148060.pdf)
- Sundials: double spherical sundial from the Delos gymnasium h 45 cm, w 32 cm, d 19 cm; hemicyclium fragment (Carrara marble) max h 19, w 22.5, d 20 cm, quadrant diameter 15 cm; hemicyclium credited to Berosus (356-323 BC). Certainty M. — [ISAW, double spherical sundial](https://isaw.nyu.edu/exhibitions/time-cosmos/objects/double-spherical-sundial); [MuviAppia hemicyclium](https://muviappia.it/en/fragment-of-spherical-hemicyclium-sundial/); [Ancient sundials of Athens](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260393038_The_ancient_Greek_Sundials_of_Athens)
- Water clocks (Classical, end 5th c. BC, Agora): terracotta clepsydra h 23.2 cm, diameter 28 cm; Agora water-clock installation square central tank ~1.68 m outside, ~0.97 m inside, hard yellow limestone. Classical. — [Odysseus](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/4/eh430.jsp?obj_id=10322); [Hesperia, Notes on a water clock](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/147833.pdf)
- Farm tools: generic sources claim sickle blades 25-50 cm, handle 20-35 cm, 400-600 g (low-grade, not Greek-specific); Iron Age ard tip 287 mm long (not Greek, not Hellenistic). Do not treat as Hellenistic measured values. — [Archaic Societies](https://archaicsocieties.com/ancient-greek-agricultural-tools/); [Sickle, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sickle)
