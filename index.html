Classical Monograph Series

Distributed Algorithms of Ancient Greece

Volume : Foundations

Allegorical retellings of the papers that founded distributed computing, in the manner of Lamport's own Part-Time Parliament. Each book is a setting in which one paper's findings are explored. Nothing is resolved and nobody wins; the arguments are given in full, and every proof waits behind a door for the reader who wants it.

The Ring Road

Causality, State & Partial Synchrony

Book I · The Tally and the Column — the trading houses of the Dolphin, the Vine, and the Anchor. Serving trade requests on a strict first-come basis without clocks along a narrow cliff road where runners cannot pass, the rule of the rising number, mutual exclusion, and what no scalar tally can see.
Lamport 1978 · Fidge 1988 · Mattern 1988

Book II · The Census of the Ring Road — the customs seat and the storehouses. Counting the whole island's goods without stopping a single cart, using a runner with a red sash to record a sound snapshot and evaluate settled properties across a morning nobody lived through.
Chandy & Lamport 1985

Book III · The Convoy of Arche — the roadsteads and slipways. Three trading houses deciding whether their merchantmen should sail together across the open sea. While winter gales inflict unbounded delays, rotating coordinator rounds and majority locks preserve safety, driving consensus the moment the storm breaks.
Dwork, Lynch & Stockmeyer 1988

Mount Phyle

Consensus & Faults

Book IV · Mercenaries and Bandits — one hill, two parties, one question apiece. Below on the spurs, four mercenaries in four tents who must go in at the same gate and cannot tell a dead man from a slow bird. Above on the ramparts, four bandits who must hold the loot or scatter, and cannot tell a lying chief from a lying peer.
Fischer, Lynch & Paterson 1985 · Lamport, Shostak & Pease 1982 · Pease, Shostak & Lamport 1980

Arche is the first of the islands, carrying the foundations of time, state, synchrony, and agreement. Further volumes are in preparation: Volume II on Paxos, where a domed rotunda and an odeon explore replicated state machines and leader election, and Volume III on Antipaxos, where gossip and CRDTs explore eventual consistency without central assembly.
