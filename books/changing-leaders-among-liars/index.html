Changing Leaders Among Liars

When some machines may lie, replacing a leader has been slow and costly. Can a new leader take over as cheaply as any ordinary step, and at the pace of the messages rather than of a timer?

The same four bandits keep the same loot book on the same windy crown. This time the job passes at every entry. Each man sends his sealed vote to the holder alone, who ties three into a bundle and sends the bundle on. One more stage than before lets a new holder take over as cheaply as an old one, and go as fast as the ravens once the wind drops.

Chapter I

What a Change Cost

On the crown of Mount Phyle, Lamportos on the peak and Shostakos, Peasios and Dolevios at their posts keep one loot book, a copy at each post. One of them may lie. Every bird is sealed with an unforgeable ring, and a wind can pin the ravens down for as long as it blows. Entries are numbered by whoever holds the job, echoed twice, and stand in the same order in every honest copy; when the holder stalls, the job passes*.

That arrangement pays for two things.

Every man writes to every man. Each echo goes from each man to all the others. With four men that is a dozen birds a stage. With forty it would be over fifteen hundred.

A change of job is heavy. The new holder must gather requests from three men, each carrying sealed proof of every entry readied at his post, and send all of it on, so that each man can check his new numbering*. A change costs far more than an ordinary entry, and the more often the job passes, the more that matters.

This book keeps the crown, the book, the seals, the wind and the four men. It changes the procedure. The job passes at every entry, whether or not anyone suspects its holder, and a change of holder costs no more than an entry.

Chapter II

Votes to One Man, Bundles to All

Instead of every man echoing to every man, each man sends his sealed vote to the holder alone. When the holder has three matching votes, he ties them into a bundle and sends the bundle to everyone. A bundle of three seals proves that three men voted, and so, with one liar, that at least two honest men did.

On the crown the bundle is three birds' worth of seals, tied together. The paper does better: with a threshold seal, where each man holds a piece and any three pieces combine into one seal that anyone can check, the bundle is the size of a single seal however many men there are. The crown's rings cannot do that; they are ordinary seals. So the saving here is in birds, not in the size of what they carry. With the threshold seal, each stage costs a number of seals that grows only in step with the number of men.

Chapter III

Three Stages and a Lock

Entries now form a chain: each entry names the one before it, so an entry stands only with everything behind it. Two entries conflict if neither lies on the other's chain.

Each man keeps two bundles: the latest first bundle he has seen, and his lock, the latest second bundle he has voted on. A term goes like this.

A term Starting. Every man sends the term's holder his latest first bundle. The holder waits for three, takes the latest of them, and proposes a new entry on top of the entry it names, sending the proposal and that bundle to all.
First vote. A man votes for the proposal if it lies on the chain of the entry he is locked on, or if the bundle it carries is from a later term than his lock.
Second vote. The holder ties three first votes into a first bundle and sends it; every man keeps it and votes again.
Third vote. The holder ties three second votes into a second bundle and sends it; every man locks on it and votes again.
Standing. The holder ties three third votes into a third bundle and sends it; every man writes the entry and everything behind it into his copy.
Moving on. If a man's glass runs out before the term ends, he moves to the next term and sends its holder his latest first bundle.
Basic HotStuff, Algorithm 2: new-view, prepare, pre-commit, commit, decide; highQC; lockedQC; safeNode, Algorithm 1; nextView.

The rule for the first vote has two halves, and each has its own job. Lies on my locked entry's chain keeps the book safe: a locked man will not help a conflicting entry forward. Carries a later bundle than my lock keeps the book moving: a man locked on something the rest have gone past can be shown that they have, and let go.

Chapter IV

Never Two Entries

Within a term, two bundles of the same stage cannot vouch for conflicting entries: each needs three seals out of four, the two threes share an honest man, and an honest man votes once a stage a term*. Across terms, the lock does the work.

Never Two Entries

However the wind blows and whoever lies, no two conflicting entries both stand at honest posts.

Proof — that the locks keep conflicting entries from both standing
  1. Suppose W stands on a third bundle from term a, and a conflicting B on one from term b. They are not the same term, so say a is earlier.
  2. B's standing needed a first bundle for B, or for something on B's chain, in term b. Among all first bundles from terms after a, up to b, for entries that conflict with W, take the one from the earliest term, s.
  3. W's third bundle in term a and that first bundle in term s each carry three seals, so they share an honest man, r. A man casts his third vote only after locking, so in term a, r locked on W's second bundle. Nothing between a and s can have moved his lock off W's chain, since that would need a first bundle for a conflicting entry earlier than s.
  4. So in term s, r was locked on W, and the proposal conflicted with W. It carried a bundle from no later than term a, again because s is the earliest. Neither half of the rule lets r vote for it. But he did vote, since his seal is in the term-s bundle. Contradiction.∎
Chapter V

The Hidden Lock

Why three stages, when the earlier arrangement made do with two echoes? Try two. A man locks as soon as he sees a first bundle, and an entry stands on the next.

In some term an entry reaches its first bundle, and only one man, say Peasios, sees it before the others' glasses run out; he locks on it. The next holder hears from the three others, not Peasios, since he need only wait for three. None of them has that bundle, so he proposes an entry that conflicts with Peasios's lock. The two other honest men vote for it. Peasios refuses: it conflicts with his lock and carries no later bundle. That is two votes, not three. The glasses run out, and just then a liar adds his vote, so the holder forms a first bundle that one man sees and locks on. The next term begins the same way. It can go on for ever.

The two-stage remedy is to make each new holder wait out a full glass before proposing, long enough, once the wind drops, to be sure of hearing every man's latest bundle. That works, but it ties every change of holder to the length of a glass rather than to the flight of a raven.

With three stages the lock cannot hide. A man locks only on a second bundle, and a second bundle exists only because three men voted on a first bundle and kept it. So a lock at any honest post means at least two honest men hold the matching first bundle. A new holder who hears from any three men hears from at least one of them. He learns the latest bundle without waiting out anything.

Going at the Ravens' Pace

Once the wind has dropped, a term whose holder is honest, with every honest man in it long enough, ends with an entry standing, and it needs to wait only for the first three answers at each stage, never for a full glass.

Proof — that an honest holder after the wind drops gets his entry through
  1. Take the latest lock held by any honest man: a second bundle. It exists because three men voted on its first bundle, at least two of them honest, and each keeps it.
  2. The holder hears the latest first bundles of three men, which include at least one of those two. So he takes a bundle at least as late as every honest lock.
  3. Every honest man then votes for the proposal. A man whose lock is from the same term as the bundle the holder took is locked on that very entry, since one term has only one second bundle, and the proposal lies on its chain. Every other honest man's lock is earlier, and the proposal carries a later bundle. In still air all votes and bundles arrive, and each stage completes on three answers. So the entry stands.∎

For every honest man to be in the same term for long enough once the wind drops, the holders pass in a fixed order by term number, and each man doubles his glass whenever a term ends with nothing standing, as before*. In the end the glasses overlap for long enough under an honest holder. The paper keeps this part, which it calls the pacemaker, apart from the rest: it affects only whether entries stand, never which.

Chapter VI

A New Holder Every Entry

The three stages all look alike: the holder sends something, the men vote, the holder bundles the votes. So they can be run on top of one another. Each term the job passes to the next man, who proposes a new entry carrying the latest first bundle. A vote for it is also a second vote for the entry before it, and a third vote for the one before that.

So a man reads the chain behind each new proposal. If it carries a bundle for its direct parent, the parent has its first bundle. If that parent carries one for its own direct parent, the grandparent is ready to lock on. If the grandparent carries one for its own direct parent too, that entry, the one behind all three, stands. Where a holder failed and a term left nothing, the chain has a gap, and counting starts again.

The job now passes at every entry, suspected or not. That is cheap for the same reason a change of holder is: a new holder only proposes on the latest bundle he hears of, like any other.

Chapter VII

What It Does Not Say

Nothing stands before the wind drops. Order holds in any wind. Entries stand only once it has dropped, as for any arrangement.

The bundle is small only with a threshold seal. The crown's rings make a bundle of three seals. The paper's saving in the size of what is carried needs seals that combine, which the rings are not.

A stage more. An entry takes one more stage than before to stand. Chained, the stages overlap, so a new entry still stands at every term.

Nobody learns who lies, and a liar may still file nonsense that stands in order.

Nothing follows from any entry.

The ideas, plainly

HotStuff is a Byzantine state machine replication protocol for n = 3f + 1 replicas under partial synchrony, like PBFT, with two properties together: a new leader costs no more than an ordinary step (linear view change), and after the network stabilizes a correct leader proceeds at the speed of actual messages rather than a timeout (optimistic responsiveness).

Votes go only to the leader, who combines n − f of them into a quorum certificate, one threshold signature, so each phase is linear. There are three phases where PBFT had two. Replicas lock on the second certificate, and vote for a proposal if it extends their lock or carries a certificate from a later view. Because a lock implies that f + 1 correct replicas hold the matching first certificate, a new leader hearing from any n − f replicas learns it without waiting; with two phases a lock can hide, and progress needs a wait for the maximum delay.

Because the phases are identical, they pipeline: every view proposes a new block, the leader rotates every view, and a block commits when three consecutive views extend it.
