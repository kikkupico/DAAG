Distributed Algorithms of Ancient Greece

The Books

Allegorical retellings of the papers that founded distributed computing, in the manner of Lamport's own Part-Time Parliament. Each book is a setting in which one paper's findings are explored. Nothing is resolved and nobody wins; the arguments are given in full, and every proof waits behind a door for the reader who wants it.

Arche

The harbour town

The Tally and the Column — the tally house and the column room. How the trading houses agreed on the order of their business with no clock between them, and what a single rising number can and cannot settle.
Lamport 1978 · Fidge 1988 · Mattern 1988

The Census of Moving Ships — the customs house. How a whole harbour's grain was counted without stopping a lighter, producing a total that is sound and describes a morning nobody lived through.
Chandy & Lamport 1985

Arche

Mount Phyle

Four Camps, Two Gates — the camps at the foot. Four commanders who could send word only by raven and could not tell a dead man from a slow bird, and were never obliged to decide anything at all.
Fischer, Lynch & Paterson 1985

The Ring of Five Seats — the council on the crown. A ring cut for five in which some unknown number of those seated do not say what they heard, and what it costs to agree anyway.
Lamport, Shostak & Pease 1982 · Pease, Shostak & Lamport 1980

Arche is the first of the islands. Further books are in preparation on Paxos, where a domed rotunda and an odeon stand a short walk apart, and on Antipaxos, where no assembly ever convenes.
