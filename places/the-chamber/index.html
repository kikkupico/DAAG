I

The Island of Paxos

Paxos is shaped like a Y. Two arms reach north and face each other across a bay: the western arm holds the Chamber of the Paxon Parliament, the eastern arm the city of Schedia and its Odeon. The arms join at a fork, where the hall of two doors stands, and below the fork the stem widens into the body of the island, the scholars' coast. The island road runs up the stem, forks, and runs out along both arms.

Paxos can read the sun. Unlike Arche in its trading season, the island tells time by day, and the Chamber has a meridian line. Word goes by messenger and by courier along the island's roads, and takes as long as it takes.

II

The Chamber

The Chamber is Lamport's, not ours, and it is not retold. It stands on the western arm, on the road to the harbour town at the arm's tip, looking across the bay at the Odeon of Schedia. It is drawn as a rotunda because Lamport gives it one physical property: its acoustics were poor, making oratory impossible, so legislators could communicate only by messenger. A round hall under a hard stone dome, with no podium and no head of the room, fits that description, and open doorways round the drum let legislators and messengers come and go.

The legislators are part-time. They wander in and out of the Chamber whenever they like, and any of them may be away for any length of time. Each keeps a ledger in which he writes the decrees that have passed, in order. Messengers carry word between them and take as long as they take; a messenger may even leave on a long voyage and never deliver his message. Nobody lies. The one thing the Parliament guarantees is that no two ledgers ever record different decrees under the same number, because any two majorities of legislators share a member.

The Parliament makes the law of Paxos: the western arm, the stem and the scholars' coast. Schedia, on the eastern arm, keeps its own laws, and the Parliament's decrees have never run there.

III

The Parliament Is Still Sitting

Lamport ends his paper with the Parliament's fall: a scribe's error names drowned sailors as the only legislators, government halts, and a coup and an invasion follow. Every other book in this series sets that ending aside, as a matter of convenience. In them the Parliament is still sitting, the Chamber still stands on the western arm, and the scholars of the island's coast still walk up to it.
