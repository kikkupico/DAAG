# Measured dimensions of Hellenistic Greek buildings and urban elements (for 3D modelling)

Certainty key: H = high (measured, multiple/primary-derived sources), M = medium (single secondary source), L = low/estimate. "Hell." = Hellenistic (323-31 BC) fabric. Only values found in sources fetched in this session are listed as findings; recalled-but-unverified material is confined to Gaps.

## 1. Stoa of Attalos (Athens, c. 150 BC) and other stoas

### Takeaway
The Athenian stoa is ~112 x 19.4 m, two storeys, with Doric outer / Ionic inner ground order and Ionic outer / "Pergamene" palm-capital inner upper order. Only a few column figures were retrievable (Doric d 0.72 m, axial spacing 2.42 m); column heights and shop sizes were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Facade 10.65 m high x 112.09 m long x 19.4 m wide; portico 13.3 m deep [as listed - ambiguous: probably the colonnade-plus-depth figure, not the 19.4 m total; M]. Windows 0.08 x 0.73 m [as given; likely slit windows; M]. Stairways at the ends: 28 steps, each 0.23 m high x 0.33 m wide. — [Perseus building record](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Athens%2C+Stoa+of+Attalos&object=Building)
- Lower outer Doric column diameter 0.72 m; intercolumniation 2.42 m; lower inner Ionic intercolumniation 4.86 m (about twice the Doric spacing: one Ionic column per two Doric). Whether "intercolumniation" is axial or clear is not stated. — same Perseus record
- Wikipedia gives 115 x 20 m (rounded, includes steps/end walls; M). Two storeys, each with two aisles; 21 rooms along the west wall per storey (42 shops closed rooms on ground floor reported as "42"); stairs at both ends; orders: ground Doric outer / Ionic inner; upper Ionic outer / Pergamene inner; Pentelic marble facade and columns, Piraeus limestone walls. Rooms lit by doorways and small windows in the back wall. — [Wikipedia: Stoa of Attalos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoa_of_Attalos)
- Search snippet: upper outer colonnade of Ionic "double half-columns", upper inner colonnade with palm capitals. — [Perseus record via search](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Athens%2C+Stoa+of+Attalos&object=Building)
- Pergamon theatre terrace stoa: 246.5 m long x 17.4 m wide Doric colonnaded terrace, "longest stoa in the Hellenistic world" (L-M, blog source). — [Theatre of Ancient Greece blog](https://theatreofancientgreece.blogspot.com/2025/06/the-architecture-and-archaeology-of.html)

### Inferences
- Doric spacing 2.42 / d 0.72 = 3.36 d axial (about 2.4 d clear if axial) - consistent with a slender, wide-spaced Hellenistic Doric; confirm axial/clear before using.
- Ionic inner spacing 4.86 m = exactly 2 x 2.43 m, so a 2:1 Doric-to-Ionic bay rhythm; ground storey 10.65 m facade height is the total to the parapet/roof, so each storey is roughly 4.5-5 m (estimate).

### Gaps
- Column heights, Ionic diameters, upper-order dimensions, shop (room) size and room height for the Stoa of Attalos: not found in fetchable sources (ASCSA agora pages were not fetched). Delos and Pergamon Attalid-type stoa dimensions not found.

## 2. Temples and orders

### Takeaway
Hellenistic Ionic temples reach column height about 9-10 d in practice; Vitruvius's canon is 8.5 d for eustyle (spacing 2.25 d). Measured big temples: Didyma column 19.7 m high; Magnesia stylobate 41 x 67 m; Priene column ~11.5 m.

### Cited Findings
- Didyma (Apollo; Hell. phase, unfinished): stylobate 51 x 109 m; crepidoma c. 60 x 120 m with 7 steps; dipteral Ionic, 10 x 21 columns; columns 19.70 m high; naiskos 8.24 x 14.23 m (Ionic prostyle, c. 300 BC); sekos walls to 25 m high; great door/threshold 14 m high, threshold 1.5 m above pronaos floor; pronaos 3 rows of 4 columns. — [Wikipedia: Temple of Apollo (Didyma)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_(Didyma))
- Artemision, Magnesia on the Maeander (Hermogenes, begun c. 200 BC, finished c. 130 BC): stylobate 67 x 41 m, peristasis 8 x 15 Ionic, pseudodipteral. — [tuerkei-antik / search summary](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/magnesia-maeander/) (M; column diameter and height not found)
- Temple of Athena Polias, Priene (Pytheos, c. 350-330 BC, finished/Hell. era; statue c. 158-156 BC): peristyle 6 x 11 Ionic; Attic foot of 29.46 cm used as module; cella-pronaos walls 100 Attic feet long and 32 feet apart; columns about 11.5 m high; 26 ceiling coffers each 65 cm square; statue 6.5 m. — [Wikipedia: Temple of Athena Polias (Priene)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Athena_Polias_(Priene))
- Olympieion, Athens: Cossutius (from 174 BC) Corinthian, 104 columns planned (3 rows of 8 front and back, double row of 20 on flanks); Wikipedia gives 17 m high x 2 m diameter for the Hellenistic scheme; Hadrianic completion 110.35 x 43.68 m, columns 17.25 m high x 1.70 m lower diameter (ratio about 10.1:1). Archaic platform 41 x 108 m. Wikipedia figures are conflicting/rounded (2 m vs 1.70 m); the 1.70 m is the measured completed column. — [Wikipedia: Olympieion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Olympian_Zeus,_Athens)
- Vitruvius III.3 (Ionic): intercolumniations pycnostyle 1.5 d, systyle 2 d, eustyle 2.25 d (centre 3 d), diastyle 3 d, araeostyle wider (wooden architraves); eustyle column height 8.5 d; shafts thicken as spacing widens; architrave = 1/13 of column height for columns 15-20 ft. — [Vitruvius Bk III, LacusCurtius/Penelope](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/3*.html)
- Tower of the Winds Corinthian: capitals lack volutes (Hell., c. 1st c. BC). See section 8.

### Inferences
- Didyma column H 19.7 m; with a lower diameter near 2 m (not sourced) the ratio would be about 10:1; treat as estimate.
- Priene temple: H 11.5 m is at 8.5-9 d for d about 1.3-1.35 m (estimate only, d not sourced).
- Modelling rule: Ionic 8.5-10 d (Hell. tending 9-10), Corinthian about 10 d (Olympieion measured 10.1), Doric Hell. about 6-7 d (Attalos: see above, unverified).

### Gaps
- Hellenistic Doric temple examples, entablature heights, and stylobate step heights not retrieved. Magnesia column diameter/height not retrieved (Wikipedia pages for Temple of Artemis Magnesia returned 404; a fetched tuerkei-antik page returned unrelated spam content and was discarded). Steps: standard Greek riser about 0.2-0.3 m is common knowledge but unsourced here.

## 3. Pergamon Great Altar and small altars

### Takeaway
Podium base 35.74 x 33.4 m, lower storey of almost 6 m with a ~20 m wide stair, a five-step platform around the structure, and a 2.3 m high Gigantomachy frieze.

### Cited Findings
- Base 35.74 m wide x 33.4 m deep; stairway nearly 20 m wide; podium lower level nearly 6 m high; five steps surround the base; Gigantomachy frieze 113 m long, 2.3 m high; Telephos frieze 1.58 m high; upper colonnade Ionic with widely spaced columns, narrow relative to base. — [Wikipedia: Pergamon Altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Altar)

### Gaps
- Colonnade column dimensions and small-altar dimensions not found.

## 4. Theatres

### Takeaway
Priene: orchestra 18.65 m, 47 tiers, seat height 0.40 m, proskenion 2.70 m. Epidauros orchestra 20 m. Pergamon: about 80 rows, 36-37 m rise, about 10,000 seats.

### Cited Findings
- Priene: orchestra greatest diameter 18.65 m; proskenion total height 2.70 m; logeion (stage podium) width 2.74 m; stage building length 18.41 m; seat height 0.40 m; 22 tiers lower + 25 upper = 47; five cunei, six stairways; capacity about 6,000+; passage orchestra to first row about 1.82 m. Proskenion added by c. 135 BC at latest; Doric half-columns on piers; stylobate aligned to a square inscribed in the orchestra circle. — [Perseus Priene Theater](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene,+Theater&object=Building), [Perseus-AA](https://perseus-aa.github.io/items/aa_350.html) (via search), [Priene theatre summary](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene,+Theater&object=Building)
- Epidauros (Hell. upper tier added): orchestra diameter 20 m; diazoma corridor 1.82 m wide; lower auditorium 12 wedges, upper 22; capacity 13,000-14,000; euripos drain 1.99 m wide. — [Wikipedia: Theatre of Epidaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Epidaurus)
- Pergamon: cavea 78-80 rows in 3 tiers with 3 diazomata; about 10,000 spectators; 36-37 m rise; slope reports 36 deg vs ~45 deg (sources conflict); proskenion pushed forward overlapping orchestra circle by about 7 m (23 ft), temporary wooden stage. — [Theatre of Ancient Greece blog](https://theatreofancientgreece.blogspot.com/2025/06/the-architecture-and-archaeology-of.html) (L-M)

### Gaps
- Delos theatre dimensions; Epidauros row counts and tread depth; Pergamon orchestra size; cavea radius for all. Vitruvius V.7 not fetched.

## 6. Houses (Priene prostas, Delos peristyle)

### Cited Findings
- Priene insula 47.2 x 35.4 m = 160 x 120 Ionic-based feet (3:4); 4 houses per insula, originally 8 narrow plots; house form: entrance passage, court, oikos/megaron with prostas on the north, service rooms; oikos could be two storeys. — [Priene summary](https://www.anasynthesis.co.uk/articles/priene), [Ozgenel](https://www.transanatolie.com/english/turkey/architecture/priene.htm), [Prostashaus](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostashaus)
- Priene House 33: 4th-c. prostas house expanded in the 2nd c. BC to more than twice its size. — [search result summary, Hoepfner and Schwandner 1986](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostashaus)
- Delos House of the Dolphins: peristyle with columns fluted only to half height; impluvium drains to a cistern on the south side. — [iswitzerland](http://www.iswitzerland.net/hilite-00000000001545-en.html)

### Inferences
- A 47.2 x 35.4 m insula divided into 8 plots gives plots about 11.8 x 17.7 m (estimate: 47.2/4 x 35.4/2 = 11.8 x 17.7 m, assuming the plots run across the short dimension; verify against plan) - roughly 200 m2. For 4 houses per insula about 400 m2 each.

### Gaps
- Room sizes, andron, room heights, door/window sizes, wall thickness, stairs, cistern volume, and all Delos peristyle measurements not found.

## 7. Town planning and fortifications

### Cited Findings
- Priene streets: main E-W 4.44 m typical, cross streets about 3.5 m, street at the stoa 7.36 m; Westtorstrasse 5.55-5.60 m (19 Ionic feet); street E of Prytaneion 4.30 m; others about 3.45 m (12 ft). — [Priene summary](https://www.anasynthesis.co.uk/articles/priene), [Ozgenel](https://www.transanatolie.com/english/turkey/architecture/priene.htm). Contradicted by Wikipedia (main about 6 m, cross about 3 m) — [Wikipedia: Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene); prefer the former (survey-derived).
- Priene wall 2 m thick; about 80 insulae; walled area 20-37 ha, residential about 15 ha. — [Wikipedia: Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene)
- Aigosthena: SE square tower about 20 m tall, 8.9 m per side; earliest circuit wall about 4-5 m thick (probably a plan thickness incl. fill, M); citadel about 90 x 187 m; 8 artillery towers; dated c. 343 BC or later (4th-3rd c. BC). — [kastra.eu / search summary](https://www.kastra.eu/castleen.php?kastro=aigosthena)
- Messene: circuit wall 9 km, 7-9 m high, about 30 square or horseshoe towers; gates with single limestone lintels. — [Wikipedia: Messene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messene)
- Pharos of Alexandria (3rd c. BC; figures from medieval Arab descriptions, L): 103-118 m total; square base about 30 x 30 m; octagonal middle about 16.4 m (24 ba) wide; circular top about 8.7 m diameter. — [Wikipedia: Lighthouse of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria)

### Gaps

## 8. Tower of the Winds (Athens, 1st c. BC)

- Octagonal, about 12 m high, about 8 m diameter, on three steps; two Corinthian porches (volute-less "Tower of the Winds" capital); 8 small interior columns under 4 ft high; roof of 24 trapezoidal slabs with central keystone, carved as tiles; 8 wind-god reliefs, 8 sundials, lion-head spouts. — [Wikipedia: Tower of the Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Winds) (M; dating of tower to c. 50 BC is conventional)

## 9. Roof elements, bricks, blocks

### Cited Findings
- Three systems: Corinthian, Laconian, Sicilian (Corinthian pan + Laconian cover). Corinthian pan tiles 36-117 cm long x 20-85 cm wide; Laconian pan 68-120 x 40-59 cm; cover tiles Corinthian 15-30 cm, Laconian 12-38 cm wide; thickness 1.2-4 cm; combined pan-plus-cover from Corinth Temple Hill about 30 kg; continuity of types Hellenistic to Roman. — [Barry, Roof Tiles and Urban Violence, GRBS](https://grbs.library.duke.edu/index.php/grbs/article/download/2921/5833) (via search)
- Vitruvius II.3: didoron (1 ft x 1/2 ft in this translation), pentadoron (5 palms per side, public buildings), tetradoron (4 palms, private). Palm = 1/4 foot, so about 0.37 m and 0.30 m squared bricks (Attic foot; computed). — [Vitruvius Bk II](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/2*.html)
- Priene seats and Attic-foot module: Priene temple uses Attic foot of 29.46 cm (see s.2).
- Priene insula: 120 x 160 ft = 35.4 x 47.2 m, implying a foot of about 0.295 m (Ionic/Attic at Priene) — [Priene summary](https://www.anasynthesis.co.uk/articles/priene)

### Gaps
- Typical roof pitch (15-20 deg) and antefix/ridge sizes: not confirmed in a source. Ashlar course heights not found. The ASCSA Agora tile standard article could not be fetched (certificate error).
