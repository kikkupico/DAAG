Ordering Without Clocks

The three houses share no hour and trade only by slip. Each keeps a tally that rises with every entry and jumps past any number a slip brings. With ties going to the lower house, all three keep one order, enough to share one storehouse. A column of three tallies, one per house, tells cause from coincidence. Neither sees news a goatherd carries over the mountain.

With no shared clock, Lamport clocks make a → b imply C(a) < C(b), and breaking ties by process number gives one total order, enough for mutual exclusion without a coordinator. Vector clocks capture causality exactly: a → b if and only if V(a) < V(b). Neither sees causality that travels outside the messages.

    Many Copies, Acting as One

    The houses now ask that a request confirmed before another was even sent be filled first: first by the sun, which no tally can see. The rule of the board asks that every request and every serving seem to take effect at one moment inside its span; then nobody can tell one board from three copies. The rule can be checked board by board, and it never makes one house wait on another's business.

    Linearizability: every operation appears to take effect at one instant between its invocation and its response, so real-time order is respected and no client can tell one copy from many. It is local, since a system of linearizable objects is linearizable, and nonblocking; sequential consistency is not local, and real-time and causal order are incomparable.

    Taking Stock Without Stopping

    To learn whether bandits took a cart, the houses count every jar, in the storehouses and on the tracks, without halting trade. A house counts its storehouse and sends a messenger in a red sash down each track before any more carts; a house that meets a sash counts at once if it has not; each logs the carts arriving on a track until that track's sash comes. The total describes a morning nobody lived, yet one the island could have passed through, so whatever stays true once true, such as a deficit, can be read from it.

    A process records its state and sends a marker on every outgoing FIFO channel before any other message; a process receiving its first marker records at once; each records an incoming channel until that channel's marker arrives. The snapshot is a consistent cut, perhaps never an instantaneous state but reachable from the start and leading to the end, so it decides stable properties such as deadlock or termination.

    Four processes, asynchronous channels that always deliver but take any time, and one process that may crash. All must decide the same value.

    The Limits of Agreement

    The ravens always arrive but may take any time, and one man may die without a sound. So no plan may wait for all four, and then the birds can always be timed to keep the night open. No fixed plan can be sure that anyone ever commits, even on a night when nobody dies.

    In an asynchronous system where one process may crash, no deterministic consensus protocol guarantees termination. Some initial configuration is bivalent, and any pending message can be delayed so that its delivery keeps the system bivalent. Safety is never violated; termination is what is lost, even when none actually crashes.

    Agreeing When Messages Run Late

    Agreeing by Chance

    Each man may now toss his own silver coin: owl for hold, goddess for attack. In numbered rounds he reports what he favours, then sends a second bird, sure of an answer only if three reports agree; on two sure birds he settles, on one he follows it, on none he tosses. Two sure answers never meet in one round, so the coins never divide the camp.

    Each round, a process broadcasts its preference and waits for n − t reports; it proposes a value held by a strict majority, decides on t + 1 proposals, adopts a value on one, and flips a local coin on none. The adversary schedules every message but cannot foresee a coin.

    Telling the Dead from the Slow

    Each tent keeps a slate, chalking men it thinks dead and rubbing a man out when his bird comes. Slates are often wrong; they are judged by how they chalk the dead and how they spare the living. If one living man is never chalked, the camp agrees however many die; if that holds only in the end, owners take turns as on windy nights, and more than half must live.

    Failure detectors are classified by completeness and accuracy. With S, consensus tolerates any number of crashes; with ◇S, a rotating coordinator solves it with a correct majority, and stays safe even if the detector misbehaves for ever. ◇W is the weakest detector that solves consensus, and consensus is equivalent to atomic broadcast.

    Answering While Cut Off

    The four tents now keep one standing plan that any man may move or ask about, and they would answer as one, by the rule of the board; but hawks may take any bird, cutting a tent off.     The Part-Time Parliament

    Legislators wander in and out, messengers may be slow, lost or twice delivered, and every promise goes into a scroll in indelible ink.     One Leader at a Time

    Only the legislator at the podium writes the law, and a line passes once the holder and two of the four benches hold it. When the podium falls silent, a caller raises a higher board, and whoever answers with the latest board-and-line takes the podium and sends out the whole law.     Agreeing Among Liars

    On the crown, rats run within a sandglass, but a bandit may lie, telling one post one thing and another the opposite. An honest guard cannot tell a lying chief from a lying peer, so three cannot bear one liar; four can, and seven can bear two, by relaying every order and taking the greater count. With seal rings no one can forge, the number of liars stops mattering. Nobody need ever learn who lied.

    In synchronous rounds with oral messages, Byzantine agreement needs n ≥ 3m + 1, and OM(m) reaches it in m + 1 rounds of relaying and majority vote. With unforgeable signatures, SM(m) works for any number of traitors. Both rely on detecting a missing message by the end of a round, and neither needs to identify a traitor.

    Spreading by Word of Mouth

    Libraries answer from their own copies of the compendium and agree only in the end; of two versions of an entry, the larger tally wins. Scholars in the stoa pass fresh news on until most of those they meet already know it, which leaves a few unheard; at the festival, copyists drawn by lot compare whole copies and catch the rest.     Probably Up to Date

    Each entry has three home libraries that answer for it. The writer announces a version once some of them hold it, and a reader takes the newest of the first few replies. If the two counts add to more than three, the two groups must share a library; many choose one and one, for speed. The odds of an old copy fall with every version the reader will accept and every moment after the announcement.

    With write quorum W and read quorum R over N replicas, R + W > N guarantees intersection, while partial quorums (R + W ≤ N) may read stale data. PBS gives the odds: within k versions, staleness falls as pk; in time, t-visibility comes from the WARS latency model by Monte Carlo.

    Changes That Never Clash

    An entry in the scholars' compendium is made of parts, each merged by its own rule. The comet's sightings are counted in pebbles, one column per library, each written only by its own library. Merging two boards keeps the larger pile in each column, so boards can meet in any order, any number of times, and always agree. Mistaken sightings go in black pebbles; a struck reference loses only the tags its striker saw. No board alone can keep the count from falling below nothing.

    CRDTs reach strong eventual consistency without coordination. State-based types merge by least upper bound, which is commutative, associative and idempotent; operation-based types need commuting operations delivered exactly once in causal order. Global invariants, such as a count that never drops below zero, still need coordination.

    What You Can Promise Alone

    A librarian cut off from every other library must still finish each visit. Alone, a librarian can hide unfinished corrections, show a visit's corrections all or none, and hold a reply until its letter arrives; if a reader keeps to one library, the reader also sees their own corrections. No librarian alone can stop two readers taking the last copy, or promise the newest edition.

    Highly available transactions: read committed, monotonic atomic view, cut isolation, monotonic reads and writes-follow-reads are achievable under partition; read-your-writes, PRAM and causal consistency need sticky clients; preventing lost update or write skew, and so snapshot isolation and serializability, is impossible, as is any recency bound.

    Knowing When to Wait

    A question that only grows, such as whether any copy has been made, can be answered as news comes in and is never taken back. One that rests on an absence, such as whether these are all the copies, must hear from everyone, which a librarian with no roster never can. So the oracle sends each group down the road its work's shape decides: south, to live as scholars without a roster, or north, to live as legislators who wait for every name on theirs.

    CALM: a program has a consistent, coordination-free distributed implementation if and only if it is monotone, equivalently if it can be computed without knowing the membership. Deadlock detection is monotone; garbage collection is not. A non-monotone step can be isolated and sealed with a manifest, moved off the critical path, or compensated afterwards.

    An absence needs everyone.CALM.

    The series in one breath

    Places that share no clock and talk only by messenger can still act as one. They order what happens by counting slips, not by reading the sun. They decide by waiting for most of them, never all, since any two majorities share a member, and every new attempt carries forward what most of them last held. Each liar costs three more men, unless seals expose him while time can be kept. No fixed plan is sure to finish when a silent man may only be slow, and no post cut off from the rest can both answer and be right; but whatever only grows can be merged in any order, with no one waiting at all.

    Processes that share no clock and communicate only by messages can still behave as one. Logical and vector clocks order events by causality, and linearizability defines what behaving as one copy means. Consensus waits for a quorum, never for all, because any two majorities intersect, and every new ballot, view or term carries forward the latest value a quorum accepted. Byzantine faults need n ≥ 3f + 1, unless signatures and synchronous rounds remove the bound. FLP rules out guaranteed termination for deterministic consensus with one crash in an asynchronous system, and CAP rules out availability with linearizability under partition; but monotone computations, such as CRDTs, need no coordination at all.
