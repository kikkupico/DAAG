One Change Apart

Each book in the series grows out of another by changing one thing: a wind that drops, a coin in each tent, a holder who may lie. Draw one book as a round of places and messengers, change one thing at a time, and the whole series unfolds, with four walls that no change gets through and ten threads that run from book to book.

Each paper in the series grows out of another by changing one assumption or one demand: partial synchrony, randomization, Byzantine faults. Draw one paper as a space-time diagram, change one assumption at a time, and the whole field unfolds, with four impossibility results and ten techniques that recur from paper to paper.

The series in one breath

Places that share no clock and talk only by messenger can still act as one. They order what happens by counting slips, not by reading the sun. They decide by waiting for most of them, never all, since any two majorities share a member, and every new attempt carries forward what most of them last held. Each liar costs three more men, unless seals expose him while time can be kept. No fixed plan is sure to finish when a silent man may only be slow, and no post cut off from the rest can both answer and be right; but whatever only grows can be merged in any order, with no one waiting at all.

Processes that share no clock and communicate only by messages can still behave as one. Logical and vector clocks order events by causality, and linearizability defines what behaving as one copy means. Consensus waits for a quorum, never for all, because any two majorities intersect, and every new ballot, view or term carries forward the latest value a quorum accepted. Byzantine faults need n ≥ 3f + 1, unless signatures and synchronous rounds remove the bound. FLP rules out guaranteed termination for deterministic consensus with one crash in an asynchronous system, and CAP rules out availability with linearizability under partition; but monotone computations, such as CRDTs, need no coordination at all.

Where the walk starts

Ordering Without Clocks

Three houses on a ring road share no hour and trade only by slip. Each keeps a tally that rises with every entry and jumps past any number a slip brings, so a cause always carries the smaller number; with ties going to the lower house, all three keep one order, enough to share one storehouse. A column of three tallies, one per house, tells cause from coincidence. Neither sees news a goatherd carries over the mountain.

With no shared clock, Lamport clocks make a → b imply C(a) < C(b), and breaking ties by process number gives one total order, enough for mutual exclusion without a coordinator. Vector clocks capture causality exactly: a → b if and only if V(a) < V(b), and incomparable vectors mark concurrent events. Neither sees causality that travels outside the messages.

Many Copies, Acting as One

The houses now ask that a request confirmed before another was even sent be filled first: first by the sun, which no tally can see. The rule of the board asks that every request and every serving seem to take effect at one moment inside its span, as if one steward kept one board; then nobody can tell one board from three copies. The rule can be checked board by board, and it never makes one house wait on another's business.

Linearizability: every operation appears to take effect at one instant between its invocation and its response, consistent with the object's sequential specification, so real-time order is respected and no client can tell one copy from many. It is local, since a system of linearizable objects is linearizable, and nonblocking; sequential consistency is not local, and real-time and causal order are incomparable.

Taking Stock Without Stopping

To learn whether bandits took a cart, the houses count every jar, in the storehouses and on the six tracks, without halting trade. A house counts its storehouse and sends a messenger in a red sash down each track before any more carts; a house that meets a sash counts at once if it has not; each logs the carts arriving on a track until that track's sash comes. The total describes a morning nobody lived, yet one the island could have passed through, so whatever stays true once true, such as a deficit, can be read from it.

A process records its state and sends a marker on every outgoing FIFO channel before any other message; a process receiving its first marker records at once; each records an incoming channel until that channel's marker arrives. The snapshot is a consistent cut, perhaps never an instantaneous state but reachable from the start and leading to the end, so it decides stable properties such as deadlock, termination or lost tokens.

The Limits of Agreement

Four mercenaries, out of sight of one another, must all attack or all hold, by ravens that always arrive but may take any time, and one man may die without a sound. A dead man looks exactly like a slow bird, so no plan may wait for all four, and then the birds can always be timed to keep the night open. No fixed plan can be sure that anyone ever commits, even on a night when nobody dies.

In an asynchronous system where one process may crash, no deterministic consensus protocol guarantees termination. A crashed process is indistinguishable from a slow one, some initial configuration is bivalent, and any pending message can be delayed so that its delivery keeps the system bivalent. Safety is never violated; termination is what is lost, even when only one process must decide and none actually crashes.

The Limits of Agreement, with one change: a wind that dropsdelays bounded after an unknown time

Agreeing When Messages Run Late

On windy nights a raven may sit in the pines for any time, but the wind always drops, at a moment no one sees, and a bird let go in still air arrives within a glass. Turns of four glasses pass from tent to tent: the turn's owner calls an answer three men can accept, and commits on two pledges, which lock the other answer out. The camp is safe in any wind and commits soon after the wind drops.

Partial synchrony: message delay is unbounded until an unknown global stabilization time and bounded after it. A rotating coordinator proposes a value acceptable to n − t processes and decides on t + 1 acknowledgments; locks, released only for later locks, keep agreement under any delay, and every correct process decides within a bounded number of rounds after stabilization.

The Limits of Agreement, with one change: a coin in each tentlocal randomness

Agreeing by Chance

Each man may now toss his own silver coin: owl for hold, goddess for attack. In numbered rounds he reports what he favours, then sends a second bird, sure of an answer only if three reports agree; on two sure birds he settles, on one he follows it, on none he tosses. Two sure answers never meet in one round, so the coins never divide the camp, and sooner or later they land alike: every man commits, with probability one.

Each round, a process broadcasts its preference and waits for n − t reports; it proposes a value held by a strict majority, decides on t + 1 proposals, adopts a value on one, and flips a local coin on none. Agreement holds on every run and termination with probability 1. The adversary schedules every message but cannot foresee a coin.

Agreeing When Messages Run Late, with one change: a slate in place of the winda failure detector in place of a timing assumption

Telling the Dead from the Slow

Each tent keeps a slate, chalking men it thinks dead and rubbing a man out when his bird comes. Slates are often wrong; they are judged by how they chalk the dead and how they spare the living. If one living man is never chalked, the camp agrees however many die; if that holds only in the end, owners take turns as on windy nights, and more than half must live. Bad slates can stall the camp, never split it.

Failure detectors are classified by completeness (crashed processes are eventually suspected) and accuracy (correct ones are spared). With S, consensus tolerates any number of crashes; with ◇S, a rotating coordinator solves it with a correct majority, and stays safe even if the detector misbehaves for ever. ◇W is the weakest detector that solves consensus, and consensus is equivalent to atomic broadcast.

Answering While Cut Off

The four tents now keep one standing plan that any man may move or ask about, and they would answer as one, by the rule of the board; but hawks may take any bird, cutting a tent off. A cut-off tent that must answer cannot also answer as one, for it cannot tell a night when the plan was moved from one when it was not. A muster-master keeps the camp as one and leaves the cut-off man waiting; waiting a glass answers everyone, and resending heals the camp within a span.

A read/write register under message loss cannot be both linearizable and available. A single primary gives consistency without availability; answering locally gives availability without consistency. With known delay bounds, timeouts give consistency in every loss-free run and Delayed-t consistency once losses stop; in an asynchronous network even loss-free runs can be stale.

Agreeing Among Liars

On the crown, rats run within a sandglass and nobody dies, but a bandit may lie, telling one post one thing and another the opposite. An honest guard cannot tell a lying chief from a lying peer, so three cannot bear one liar; four can, and seven can bear two, by relaying every order and taking the greater count. With seal rings no one can forge, the number of liars stops mattering. Nobody need ever learn who lied.

In synchronous rounds with oral messages, Byzantine agreement needs n ≥ 3m + 1, and OM(m) reaches it in m + 1 rounds of relaying and majority vote. With unforgeable signatures, SM(m) works for any number of traitors. Both rely on detecting a missing message by the end of a round, and neither needs to identify a traitor.

The Part-Time Parliament

Legislators wander in and out, messengers may be slow, lost or twice delivered, and every promise goes into a scroll in indelible ink. Any two majorities share a legislator, so each numbered ballot first asks a majority for their latest votes and a promise to ignore lower ballots, then proposes the decree of the latest vote among them. One president runs ballots for every decree number at once; two presidents only slow things down.

Paxos: numbered ballots with majority quorums. Phase 1 (prepare and promise) learns the highest-numbered accepted value; phase 2 (accept) proposes it. It is safe under any delay, loss, duplication, crash or recovery, and live when one leader and a majority stay up long enough. Multi-Paxos runs phase 1 once for every open log slot, filling gaps with no-ops, and leases make reads linearizable.

One Leader at a Time

Only the legislator at the podium writes the law, and a line passes once the holder and two of the four benches hold it. When the podium falls silent, a caller raises a higher board, and whoever answers with the latest board-and-line takes the podium and sends out the whole law. A holder left on the porch can write, never pass. Wax is fast but forgets: if most go home at once, the council can stop for good, never wrong.

Viewstamped Replication: a primary orders operations and commits once a majority holds them. On a view change, the member of a responding majority with the highest viewstamp becomes primary and sends its whole state; a deposed primary cannot commit. State is volatile, so view-formation rules guard against lost logs, and a simultaneous crash of a majority halts the group rather than losing a commit.

One Leader at a Time, with one change: the holder may liea Byzantine primary

Keeping Order Among Liars

Back on the crown, on windy nights now, the four keep one loot scroll, a copy at each post, and the registrar who numbers its entries may lie. An entry stands only after two echoes, each gathering three seals of four; any two groups of three share an honest man, so no number can hold two entries. A registrar who stalls loses the job to the next post, who must keep every entry readied before.

PBFT: 3f + 1 replicas; the primary assigns sequence numbers; pre-prepare, prepare and commit each need 2f + 1 matching signed messages, and any two such quorums share a correct replica. A view change carries prepared certificates, so committed requests survive; doubling timeouts give liveness once delays stop growing; stable checkpoints bound the log.

Keeping Order Among Liars, with one change: votes tied into bundlesvotes aggregated into certificates

Changing Leaders Among Liars

Instead of every bandit writing to every other, each sends his sealed vote to the registrar, who ties three into one bundle. A third stage of bundles means any man's lock is backed by two honest men holding its first bundle, so a new registrar learns of it from the first three replies and goes at the rats' pace, not a sandglass's. A change of hands now costs no more than an entry, so the job can pass at every entry.

HotStuff: replicas vote only to the leader, which aggregates 2f + 1 votes into a quorum certificate, so each phase is linear. A third phase makes the view change linear and optimistically responsive: a locked QC implies f + 1 correct replicas hold its prepare QC, so a new leader learns it from any n − f replies. Chained HotStuff pipelines the phases and rotates the leader every block.

Answering While Cut Off, with one change: copies may differ for a whileeventual consistency

Spreading by Word of Mouth

Libraries answer from their own shelves and agree only in the end; of two editions, the larger tally wins. Scholars in the stoa pass fresh news on until most of those they meet already know it, which leaves a few unheard; at the festival, copyists drawn by lot compare whole collections and catch the rest. A withdrawal must travel as a notice, or an old copy brings the work back.

Epidemic replication with last-writer-wins timestamps. Rumor mongering is fast and cheap but leaves a residue of about e−m sites for m messages per site; anti-entropy, pairwise reconciliation with random partners, reaches every site, and pull beats push in the tail. Deletions spread as death certificates, kept dormant at a few sites.

Spreading by Word of Mouth, with one change: ask only some of the copiespartial quorums

Probably Up to Date

A work lives at three home libraries. The writer announces an edition once some of them hold it, and a reader takes the newest of the first few replies. If the two counts add to more than three, the two groups must share a library; many choose one and one, for speed. The odds of an old copy fall with every edition the reader will accept and every moment after the announcement.

With write quorum W and read quorum R over N replicas, R + W > N guarantees intersection, while partial quorums (R + W ≤ N) may read stale data. PBS gives the odds: within k versions, staleness falls as pk; in time, t-visibility comes from the WARS latency model by Monte Carlo. Staleness is usually brief, though unbounded in the worst case.

Changes That Never Clash

Scribes count copies in pebbles, one column per terrace, each written only by its own scribe. Merging two tables keeps the larger pile in each column, so tables can meet in any order, any number of times, and always agree. Losses go in black pebbles; a struck title loses only the tags its striker saw. No table alone can keep a count from falling below zero.

CRDTs reach strong eventual consistency without coordination. State-based types merge by least upper bound, which is commutative, associative and idempotent; operation-based types need commuting operations delivered exactly once in causal order. G- and PN-counters, LWW and multi-value registers, 2P and OR sets. Global invariants, such as a count that never drops below zero, still need coordination.

Answering While Cut Off, with one change: whole visits, while cut offtransactions under partition

What You Can Promise Alone

A librarian cut off from every other library must still finish each visit. Alone, he can hide unfinished corrections, show a visit's corrections all or none, and hold a reply until its letter arrives; if a reader keeps to one library, the reader also sees their own corrections. No librarian alone can stop two readers taking the last copy, or promise the newest edition.

Highly available transactions: read committed, monotonic atomic view, cut isolation, monotonic reads and writes-follow-reads are achievable under partition; read-your-writes, PRAM and causal consistency need sticky clients; preventing lost update or write skew, and so snapshot isolation and serializability, is impossible, as is any recency bound.

Changes That Never Clash, with one change: from data that only grows to questions that only growfrom monotone data to monotone programs

Knowing When to Wait

A question that only grows, such as whether any copy has been made, can be answered as news comes in and is never taken back. One that rests on an absence, such as whether these are all the copies, must hear from everyone, which a librarian with no roster never can. So the hall sends each question out by the door its shape decides: south to the coast, or north to the assemblies.

CALM: a program has a consistent, coordination-free distributed implementation if and only if it is monotone, equivalently if it can be computed without knowing the membership. Deadlock detection is monotone; garbage collection is not. A non-monotone step can be isolated and sealed with a manifest, moved off the critical path, or compensated afterwards.

Part two

Four walls

No change of procedure gets through these four. Each is proved the same way: two nights that look the same from one post, yet call for different answers. Scroll through a wall and the round shows one night, then the other; the gold stretch is what the post sees, and it never changes.

Four impossibility results, each proved by indistinguishability: two executions that look the same to one process yet require different outputs. Scroll through each and the diagram shows one execution, then the other; the gold stretch is the process's view, and it never changes.

Wall one

No plan is sure to finishFLP

When birds may take any time and one man may fall silent, no fixed plan can be sure that anyone ever commits. The night is never decided wrongly; it may simply never be decided, though no one dies. A wind that drops, a coin in each tent, or a slate of suspects gets the camp through.

Deterministic consensus in an asynchronous system with one crash cannot guarantee termination, though safety is never lost. Partial synchrony, randomization and failure detectors each circumvent it.

Wall two

Half cannot speak for allQuorum bounds

If half the camp may fall silent, a wind can hold every bird between the halves until each half commits alone, so more than half must live, with coins or slates alike; only a slate that never chalks one living man escapes. If men may lie, three cannot bear one liar: four can bear one, seven two. Seals lift that count only while silence can be timed; in the wind it takes four to bear one liar, sealed or not.

With crash faults, partial synchrony, randomization and eventually accurate detectors all need n ≥ 2t + 1 (with S, any number may crash). Byzantine agreement needs n ≥ 3m + 1 with oral messages; signatures remove the bound only in synchronous rounds; under partial synchrony n ≥ 3t + 1, signed or not.

Wall three

Answer, or be rightCAP

A tent cut off by hawks cannot both answer every question and answer as one. With a known flight, waiting a glass keeps the copies as one on every night no bird is lost; when birds may also be late without limit, not even then. So no librarian alone can stop two readers taking the last copy, or promise the newest edition.

Under message loss, a register cannot be both linearizable and available; with bounded delays, consistency survives in loss-free runs; asynchronously, not even then. Preventing lost update or write skew, and any recency guarantee, are therefore unavailable.

Wall four

An absence needs everyoneCALM

A question that only grows needs no waiting and no roster; a question that rests on none, every or only must hear from everyone first. That is why an old notice of withdrawal can be thrown away only when every library has been heard from, why no table alone keeps a count above zero, and why nothing on the coast that only grows ever waits for an assembly.

Exactly the non-monotone computations, such as negation, aggregation, global invariants and garbage collection, need coordination; monotone ones run coordination-free, and so stay available under partition.
