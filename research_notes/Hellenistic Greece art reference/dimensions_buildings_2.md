## 1. Vitruvius proportion rules (Roman author, c. 25 BC, codifying Hellenistic Greek practice)

### Takeaway
Vitruvius Doric column = 14 modules = 7 lower diameters (module = half diameter); Corinthian capital height = lower shaft diameter; doors are derived from cella height (Doric door height = 2/3.5 of floor-to-ceiling; width = 5.5/12 of door height; Ionic width = 1.5/2.5 of height as translated).

### Cited Findings
- Doric column (IV.3): thickness 2 modules, height 14 modules (so H = 7 d); capital 1 module high, split into abacus, echinus, hypotrachelium; 20 flutes. — [Vitruvius Bk IV (Gwilt transl.), Penelope/Thayer](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/4*.html)
- Corinthian (IV.1): capital height including abacus = lower shaft diameter (Ionic capital = 1/3 d); abacus diagonal = 2 x its height; abacus faces curve in 1/9 of their extent. — same
- Doors, Doric/Attic (IV.6): height pavement-to-lacunaria divided into 3.5 parts, 2 parts = door height; door height divided into 12, 5.5 parts = width at the bottom. Taper (door narrower at top): height under 16 ft: top narrowed by 1/3 of dressing width; 16-25 ft: 1/4; 25-30 ft: 1/8; above 30 ft: vertical sides. Dressing (jamb) width = 1/12 of door height; architrave height = upper width of dressing. — same
- Ionic doors: height as Doric; width = height divided into 2.5, 1.5 parts for the width below (as translated; this gives a very wide door, 0.6 H, and the verbatim translation should be checked against the Latin before use); dressing width = 1/14 of aperture height. — same
- Cella (IV.4): temple length 2x its width; cella (incl. walls) one-fourth longer than wide. — same
- Foot-based conversions: Vitruvius foot = Roman foot (about 0.296 m) vs Attic/Ionic foot about 0.295-0.296 m used at Priene (see earlier file, 29.46 cm); the two are practically identical, so Vitruvian feet can be used directly.

### Inferences
- Worked door example: temple interior (floor to ceiling) 7 m -> door height 4.0 m, width 1.83 m (Doric rule). A house door, which these rules do not cover, is not given by Vitruvius.
- Doric Vitruvian 7 d is more stocky than the Hellenistic examples below (Pergamon Athena 7.0 d; Delos Philip stoa and Attalos see section 3), so 7 d is the right default for temples; stoas about 6.5-7.5 d.

### Gaps
- Vitruvius Ionic base/entablature rules (III.5) and Corinthian entablature not fetched.

## 2. Priene: public buildings and town

### Takeaway
Agora 76 x 35 m, lower gymnasium palaestra about 35 x 34 m, stadium track about 191 x 20 m. Street widths still conflicted: survey sources 4.44/3.5 m vs Wikipedia 6/3 m.

### Cited Findings
- Agora (3rd c. BC): rectangular, 76 x 35 m (search snippet; the figure is plausible with the 7.36 m street at the stoa/market from the first file; M). — [sacred-destinations / Perseus family](https://www.sacred-destinations.com/turkey/priene) (via search; Perseus pages 503)
- Lower gymnasium (c. 130 BC): palaestra 35.11 x 34.35 m. — [Perseus Priene Lower Gymnasium](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene,+Lower+Gymnasium&object=Building) (search snippet; fetch 503)
- Stadium (c. 130 BC, with the lower gymnasium): track about 191 m long, about 20 m wide. — [Perseus Priene Stadium](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene%2C+Stadium&object=Building) (search snippet; fetch 503)
- Alexandreion (Prytaneion/Alexander-temple precinct building, Priene): whole complex 20.50 x 35.40 m; north room 19 x 9.20 m; door width 2.25 m. — [Perseus Priene Alexandreion](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene,+Alexandreion&object=Building) (search snippet; M)
- Priene wall 2 m thick (Hell., c. 350-330 BC fabric) — [Wikipedia: Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene). Wikipedia streets: 6 main about 6 m, 15 cross about 3 m.
- Street-width conflict: Wikipedia (6/3 m) is a rounded popular figure; the survey-derived figures (4.44 typical E-W, 3.5 cross, 5.55-5.6 Westtorstrasse = 19 ft, 7.36 m near stoa) are in the first file from the Ozgenel/Hoepfner-Schwandner line. Verdict: the actual main streets are 4.4-5.6 m (14-19 Ionic ft) with 7.4 m at the agora; cross streets 3.5 m (12 ft). Wikipedia's "about 6 m" matches only the widest (Westtorstrasse). Model 4.5 m main / 3.5 m cross, widen to 5.6 m at the west gate, 7.4 m at the agora. (resolution is an inference; M)
- Priene house typology (Hoepfner-Schwandner): insula of 4 houses (originally 8 plots), prostas house with oikos on the north. Room-size numbers were not recoverable from the sources reached (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Priene house room sizes (oikos/andron/court), wall thickness, door/window sizes, room heights; sanctuary of Athena precinct dimensions; gate dimensions; tower sizes. Hoepfner and Schwandner 1986 (Haus und Stadt im klassischen Griechenland) is the primary source and was not accessible. Perseus and the irbnet/WIT pdf results (irbnet.de 06059012922.pdf; witpress STR11006) were found but not fetched in this pass.

## 3. Delos

### Takeaway
House of the Dolphins ground floor 404 m2 with a 12-column peristyle. Theatre: orchestra 21.2 m, cavea 65 m wide, 26 + 18 rows. Doric stoas: Philip V 71.08 x 10.71 m (d 0.91, spacing 3.35); South Stoa 72.47 x 12.9 m (d about 0.65, spacing about 2.48).

### Cited Findings
- House of the Dolphins: ground-floor area 404 m2 (12th largest at Delos); peristyle of twelve columns. — [Biblioscout, Delos Beyond East and West (via search snippet)](https://biblioscout.net/book/chapter/10.25162/9783515129268/00027) (M)
- Delos theatre (begun soon after 314 BC, finished about 70 years later): cavea width 65 m; ima cavea 26 rows in 7 cunei; summa cavea 18 rows at widest; orchestra diameter 21.20 m (about 19 m at the drain); capacity quoted 5,500 vs 6,500 (conflict). — [Diazoma: Theatre of Delos](https://diazoma.gr/en/theaters/theatre-of-delos/) (via search; M)
- Stoa of Philip V (216-200 BC): east stoa 71.08 x 10.71 m, Doric d 0.91 m, intercolumniation 3.35 m (axial vs clear not stated; 3.35/0.91 = 3.7 d axial, or about 3.7 d clear if clear), 16 columns on the facade, end walls with 4 windows each. — [Perseus Delos Stoa of Philip](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Delos,+Stoa+of+Philip&object=Building) (search snippet)
- South Stoa (3rd c. BC, Doric, 28 columns): 72.47 x 12.9 m, d about 0.65 m, intercolumniation about 2.48 m. — [Perseus Delos South Stoa](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Delos,+South+Stoa&object=Building) (search snippet)
- Hypostyle Hall (Hall of the Poseidoniasts? / Hypostyle, c. 210 BC): 56.44 x 34.28 m, nearly open on the south. — [Classical Monuments tumblr](https://www.tumblr.com/classicalmonuments/614599351238574080/hypostyle-hall-stoa-of-poseidon-delos-greece) (L)
- Cisterns: Delos private house cisterns rock-cut, estimated about 10 m3 each; the large public cistern near the theatre is 22.5 x 6 m, 8.3 m deep, about 270 m3. — [Cistern Delos, Antoniou in Water 2013 5(4) via search](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/5/4/1916); [Central cistern figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Central-cistern-on-island-of-Delos-by-GP-Antoniou_fig3_264313553) (M; 22.5 x 6 x 8.3 = 1,120 m3 so the 270 m3 figure cannot be the full box - likely a sub-chamber or sloped; treat as conflicting)

### Inferences
- Doric stoa heights from Vitruvian 7 d: Philip V d 0.91 -> H about 6.4 m; South Stoa d 0.65 -> about 4.5 m. These are estimates, not measured heights.

### Gaps
- Delos House of Trident, Masks, Hill House dimensions; the Delos theatre stage/proskenion; wall thickness; Delos stoa column heights.

## 6. Stoas, Priene Sacred Stoa (best measured Hellenistic stoa found)

### Takeaway
Priene Sacred Stoa (c. 160-150 BC, Ariarathes VI): 116.46 m long x 11.81 m deep, Doric outer 5.20 m high on d 0.70 m (7.4 d), entablature 1.52 m, Ionic inner d 0.776 m, H 5.51 m (7.1 d); shops 4.40 x 3.62 m. This is the dimension set to copy for a stoa.

### Cited Findings
- Sacred Stoa, Priene: two-aisled; length 116.46 m; portico depth 11.81 m; Doric outer colonnade 49 columns, d 0.70 m, axial spacing 2.32 m, H 5.20 m, entablature 1.52 m; Ionic inner colonnade 24 columns, d 0.776 m, axial spacing 4.65 m, H 5.51 m (inner spacing = 2 x outer, as at Attalos); 15 shops along the north (rear) wall, extending about 76 m, each about 4.40 m wide x 3.62 m deep. — [Perseus Priene Sacred Stoa](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene%2C+Sacred+Stoa&object=Building) (via search snippet; M-H)
- Priene agora (3rd c. BC) occupies two insulae: 75.63 x 35.40 m, stoas on three sides in the Doric order, shops, offices; main street before the Sacred Stoa 7.36 m wide. — [Cordan and Besgen, No Times But Principles (Priene)](https://papers.cumincad.org/data/works/att/ddss9813.content.pdf) (full-text read; M). Agrees with search figure 76 x 35 m above (75.63 = 2 insulae of 35.4 less/plus streets); resolved: 75.6 x 35.4 m.
- Priene Athena sanctuary: temple stylobate 37.17 x 19.53 m (6 x 11); altar 13.20 x 7.12 x 3 m high (mid-2nd c. BC); south stoa 78.40 m long x 6.80 m deep, one aisle, 32 Doric columns; propylon 7.60 m wide outside-to-outside, court 7.30 m long, south wall preserved to about 4.5 m (propylon Augustan, Roman). — [Perseus Priene Stoa of Athena Sanctuary](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene%2C+Stoa+of+Athena+Sanctuary&object=Building), [Perseus Propylon](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Priene%2C+Propylon+of+Athena+Sanctuary&object=Building), [Classical Monuments: Altar](https://www.tumblr.com/classicalmonuments/612878116891836416/altar-of-athena-polias-priene-ionia-turkey-325), [Propylon](https://www.tumblr.com/classicalmonuments/612968595997294592/propylon-of-the-athena-polias-sanctuary-priene) (search snippets; M-L)
- Priene Egyptian gods sanctuary (3rd c. BC): temenos 47 x 31 m; altar 14.60 x 7.31 m. — [Classical Monuments](https://www.tumblr.com/classicalmonuments/613874577984061440/sanctuary-of-egyptian-gods-priene-ionia-turkey) (L)
- Priene stadium: spectator seating one side only; 6 m wide walkway above the seats the full 190 m, with a stoa of the same length beside it. — [Cordan and Besgen](https://papers.cumincad.org/data/works/att/ddss9813.content.pdf) (M)
- Pergamon: upper gymnasion terrace 150 x 70 m (other sources 200 x 45 m: conflict); Demeter sanctuary 50 x 110 m, Ionic temple 6.45 x 12.7 m; upper agora with Doric stoas and small Hermes temple (2nd half 2nd c. BC). — [Nomadic Niko / sources via search](https://nomadicniko.com/2022/06/09/pergamon-sanctuary-of-demeter/) (L-M)

### Inferences
- Stoa rules from Priene: Doric 7.4 d (more slender than Vitruvius 7 d), entablature = 1.52/5.20 = 0.29 of column height; spacing 3.3 d axial; Ionic 7.1 d (short, because under a continuous roof); module 4.65 m bay. Attalos (Athens) bay 2.42 m on d 0.72 is similar. For the Delos Philip stoa spacing/d = 3.7. Shop 4.4 x 3.6 m, about 16 m2.
- Hellenistic Doric column heights in stoas: about 5.2-5.5 m (7 d), not the 6.4 m guessed in the Delos inference above (Priene is the measured case; Delos d 0.91 -> Delos stoa H estimate about 6.4 m at 7 d).

## 7. Houses (Priene, Olynthos comparison), doors, walls

### Cited Findings
- Priene: houses are one or two storeys; ground floors partly cut from rock; ordinary houses rubble-and-mortar walls, some rendered with brown coat; brick walls rare; storey heights NOT known (the source says so); roofs tiled on timber. Windows in non-court rooms are high above eye level, unglazed, closed with tablet shutters with arch-shaped holes; doors single leaf generally, monumental ones double leaf; entrance passage/prothyron recessed up to 1 m; the Great House entrance corridor 2 m wide, front 35 m wide; 26 rooms (16 women's, 10 men's) after merging with its eastern neighbour and absorbing the 3.50 m street (Hell., 2nd c. BC). — [Cordan and Besgen](https://papers.cumincad.org/data/works/att/ddss9813.content.pdf) (M, full-text read)
- Priene insula 47.2 x 35.4 m; (another paper gives 48.8 x 36.5 m including streets, 8 houses per lot). — [Kulozu and Acmaz, METU](https://www.irbnet.de/daten/iconda/06059012922.pdf) (read); [Cordan and Besgen](https://papers.cumincad.org/data/works/att/ddss9813.content.pdf)
- Olynthos (Classical, 5th-4th c. BC, comparison only): houses about 15 x 15 m with about six rooms and a court; House A vii 4 about 17.2 x 17.0 m; mudbrick about 0.49 x 0.49 x 0.09 m on a low stone socle. — [Wikipedia: Olynthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olynthus), [Perseus A vii 4](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Olynthus,+House+A+vii+4&object=Building) (via search; M; Classical)
- Delos House of the Dolphins: 404 m2 ground floor (see section 3). House of the Masks (c. 120-80 BC): several courtyards, painted marble-imitation walls, four mosaic rooms. — [Perseus Delos House of the Masks](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifact?name=Delos%2C+House+of+the+Masks&object=building) (read; no dimensions)

### Inferences
- Door heights: not found. Use Vitruvius (section 1): e.g. a 3.5 m court-room ceiling gives Doric-type door height 2.0 m and width 0.92 m (about 3:7). The Priene source stating windows are high on the street front justifies a blind lower street wall of about 4 m with no openings (estimate).

### Gaps
- Priene room sizes, wall thickness, ceiling/storey heights, door/window dimensions: not in any retrievable source (Hoepfner-Schwandner 1986 or Wiegand-Schrader 1904 needed). Delos Trident, Masks, Hill House areas not found (Perseus pages 503 or without data).
