# Hellenistic Greek clothing, footwear, hair and personal appearance (323-31 BC)

Method note: web fetches of the Met, Walters and Commons category pages mostly failed (429/403/404), and several Wikipedia fetches returned thin summaries. Cited findings below are only what the retrieved pages actually said. Items marked [BG] are standard background knowledge (Bieber, Lee, Pollitt) that I did NOT verify in this session; the report writer should treat them as unverified. Period flags: [CLASSICAL], [ROMAN], [HELL].

## Men's garments and headgear (chiton, exomis, himation, chlamys, petasos, pilos, kausia, tribon)

### Takeaway
Men wore a chiton (short for work/travel), with the himation as the citizen's outer wrap, the chlamys for travel/military/hunting, and the exomis for labourers. Hats: petasos (rural/travel), pilos/pileus (close cap), kausia (Macedonian flat brim, Hellenistic marker).

### Cited Findings
- Chiton: light linen tube secured at the shoulders, worn by both sexes; athletes and workers wore shorter versions. Peplos is pinned wool with an overfold (apoptygma) and belt. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)
- Himation: heavy rectangular wool wrap, passing under the left arm and secured at the right shoulder (one common arrangement); worn by both sexes; women pulled it over the head for weddings/funerals. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)
- Himation drapes in art vary; most common has the upper corner on the left shoulder, bulk wrapped round the back, passed under the right arm, or draped over/crossed over the left arm. — [search summary citing FIT](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/himation/)
- Aeschines portrait (original c. 320-310 BC; the surviving statue is a c. 50 AD Roman copy): chiton + himation + sandals, right arm tucked across chest in the drapery, left hand on hip within the drapery; the modest pose signals self-control. Wearing chiton under himation in art is unusual before the Hellenistic period, so this is one of the earliest examples of the new trend (arm-bound himation, often worn alone over bare torso by philosophers/older citizens in [BG]). — [Digital Portrait Basket, ARTH488A](https://classicalchopped2.artinterp.org/omeka/items/show/27)
- Chlamys: seamless wool rectangle for military/hunting, brooch at right shoulder, typical 5th-3rd c. BC. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing). Hermes' attributes: kerykeion, winged boots, brimmed petasos, traveller's chlamys. — [Hermes (search summary of Wikipedia/Theoi)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermes)
- Exomis: short belted tunic fastened on one shoulder leaving the other arm free; in art worn by artisans, labourers, slaves, soldiers; also on Hellenistic/Roman bronze fishermen figurines. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing); [search result on exomis/fishermen](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:The_Old_Fisherman_(Louvre_Ma_1354))
- Hellenistic genre sculpture shows fishermen, shepherds, farmers, market women, beggars with emphasis on physical decay (the Old Fisherman, Louvre Ma 1354, is a Roman copy of a Hellenistic original). — [Villa Chiragan, Musée Saint-Raymond](https://villachiragan.saintraymond.toulouse.fr/en/partie-03-l-art-grec-revisite/ra-46-vieux-pecheur)
- Petasos: broad-brimmed hat of Thessalian origin with chin cord; worn by farmers, travellers, hunters, considered rural; felt, leather, straw or skin; the winged petasos is Hermes' symbol; a bronze petasos helmet existed for Athenian cavalry. — [Wikipedia: Petasos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petasos)
- Pilos/pileus: close-fitting cap of leather or wool; tribon: simple cloak of Spartans and Cynic philosophers. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)
- Kausia: flat, broad-brimmed Macedonian felt hat; purple kausia with diadem was royal regalia; also worn by Macedonian soldiers and ordinary Greeks (terracottas); coins/statues from Mediterranean to Greco-Bactria. Commons files named on the page: Macedonian_boy_BM_1906.10-19.1.jpg, Terrakota_Statue_eines_Makedoniers_3_Jhdt_v_Chr.jpg, Ancient_Macedonian_soldiers_from_the_tomb_of_Agios_Athanasios_Greece.jpg. — [Wikipedia: Kausia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kausia)
- Theophrastus (Characters, c. 320 BC) lampoons the boor: hob-nailed boots, shoes too big, cloak not reaching the knee. — [Theophrastus Characters (search summary)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/58242/58242-h/58242-h.htm)

### Inferences
- A Hellenistic male citizen reads as: knee/shin-length wool or linen chiton, ample himation, sandals; a traveller as chlamys + petasos (often hanging on the back by its cord) + krepides/endromides + staff [BG]. A messenger fitted to the project's canon (bare-headed, per recent repo commits) is a deliberate departure from typical petasos iconography; note that the Hermes/herald link supports the petasos for travellers.
- Kausia is the best single "Macedonian/Hellenistic" visual cue; avoid it on mainland Greek civilians unless the figure is Macedonian-linked.

### Gaps
- No verified source fetched on tribon colour/size, himation sizes, or the "himation with no chiton" philosopher/orator look beyond Aeschines. [BG] only.
- Bronze/metal details of fibulae and pera (satchel) not found in this session; pera is attested for travellers, Cynics (Diogenes) and shepherds [BG, unverified].

## Footwear

### Takeaway
Sandals were standard; krepides were more enclosed, laced shoes; endromides were high flexible boots for travel/hunting, mostly associated with gods and heroes in art.

### Cited Findings
- Sandals: leather sole with straps between toes, over instep or round ankle; krepides: laced shoes/sandals, often open-toed with heel or side covering, not a single pattern. — [x-legio: Greek shoes](https://x-legio.com/en/wiki/greek-shoes)
- Endromides: high flexible boots for riding, hunting, travel; artists put them on Dionysus, Heracles, Perseus, so they do not necessarily reflect civilian wear. — [x-legio](https://x-legio.com/en/wiki/greek-shoes)
- Wealthy wearers: coloured leather, metal fittings, embroidery, inlay; black, red, yellow, white shoes attested in texts/art but art can't prove prevalence; eastern soft shoes with upturned toes and Persian-style ankle-fastened shoes appear. — [x-legio](https://x-legio.com/en/wiki/greek-shoes)
- Greeks typically went barefoot indoors; Iphicrates designed light, easily untied soldiers' boots. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)
- Hermes' boots in early vase painting have a curved pull-strap that later becomes wings. — [Talaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaria)
- Note x-legio is a reenactor/hobby site: moderate reliability.

### Inferences
- Tanagra-type women: thick-soled, closed-looking shoes peeking from under the hem [BG]. Barefoot is acceptable for slaves/rustics.

### Gaps
- Could not open Athanasopoulou et al. (NAMA footwear, academia.edu) for dated Hellenistic examples.

## Beards and hair (men)

### Takeaway
Beards were the classical norm; after Alexander clean-shaven became the elite fashion, leaving the beard as a mark of philosophers (Cynics, Stoics), older men, and rustics.

### Cited Findings
- Alexander favoured a clean-shaven face; Plutarch says he ordered soldiers shaved so enemies couldn't seize beards; shaving spread among elites; beards became the "philosopher's" trait. — [The Collector: Beards in ancient Greece](https://www.thecollector.com/beards-ancient-greece/); [Money Museum: Fashion on Coins I: Beards](https://www.moneymuseum.com/pdf/yesterday/02_Accross_the_Times/08%20Fashion%20on%20Coins%20I%20Beards.pdf)
- Cynics and Stoics kept beards as austerity; Lucian mocked "a goat with a true beard is at once a complete Plato" (Lucian is 2nd c. AD, ROMAN-era commentary). — [The Collector](https://www.thecollector.com/beards-ancient-greece/)
- Cicada hair ornaments in gold were worn by Athenian men (Archaic/Classical, [CLASSICAL]); theatre wigs: black for tyrants, fair curls for young heroes, red for deceitful slaves. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)

### Inferences
- Royal/Hellenistic-ruler portrait look: clean-shaven, strong mop of hair swept up from the forehead (anastole, Alexander's lion-like hair [BG]). Citizens of the 3rd-1st c. often short hair, clean-shaven or short beard [BG]; philosophers/old men: long full beard, cropped or untidy hair.

### Gaps
- No verified source for specific stubble/short-beard fashions, hair length of ordinary men, or hair-cutting customs (barbers' shops attested [BG]).

## Women's dress, veils, hats, fans

### Takeaway
Tanagra and related Hellenistic terracottas show women in chiton plus a thin enveloping himation (often drawn over the head), with wreaths, fans and broad sun-hats; the peplos is an older/Classical garment that persists in cult images.

### Cited Findings
- Tanagras begin in the later 4th c. BC and run through the Hellenistic period; depict elegant everyday costume: hats, wreaths, fans; himation over chiton shows the body under the folds; moulded, white slip then watercolour paint. — [Wikipedia: Tanagra figurine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanagra_figurine)
- Most Tanagras are fashionable women or girls wrapped in thin himatia, often wearing large sun hats, holding wreaths or fans. — [Tanagra search summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanagra_figurine)
- "Lady in Blue" (Louvre MNB 907), c. 330-300 BC, 32.5 cm, gilt and painted terracotta, draped in himation holding a fan; found in the Tanagra necropolis, acquired 1876. — [Louvre collections](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010280756); [File:Lady in blue Louvre MNB907.jpg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lady_in_blue_Louvre_MNB907.jpg)
- Women's petasos versions have elevated crowns (the Tanagra "tholia" hat shape: a pointed/conical straw sun hat [BG]). — [Wikipedia: Petasos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petasos)
- Himation worn by women over head as veil at weddings and funerals, folds could cover the face. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing); [mourning search results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_in_ancient_Greek_art)
- Headbands (tainia, sphendone) and hairnets (kekryphalos) confined hair. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)
- Tanagra figures typically 10-20 cm; polychrome (pink, blue, red; gilding) with ochre/coal/malachite pigments. — [Wikipedia: Greek terracotta figurines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_terracotta_figurines)

### Inferences
- Palette for women: pale blue, rose, pale yellow, white, with Tyrian-purple border for wealth; high-belted chiton under the breasts (Hellenistic "Ionic" look) with wide himation; sleeves of fine linen with button-fastened upper arm [BG, unverified].
- The Tanagra look is genteel Boeotian/Athenian; Alexandria and Asia Minor (Myrina, Smyrna) show richer, more theatrical styles [BG].

### Gaps
- No verified sources on chitoniskos for women (Artemis/Amazon short chiton [CLASSICAL/HELL]), melon veil styles, or fan material (feather/palm leaf [BG]).

## Women's hairstyles and jewellery

### Takeaway
Hair: Knidian bun, melon coiffure, lampadion topknot, Heracles-knot hairstyle; jewellery: gold diadems, garnet-inlaid Heracles knots, snake bracelets, spiral forms.

### Cited Findings
- Lampadion ("little torch") topknot named by Dicaearchus (active c. 320-300 BC); melon (peponoeidis) coiffure has parallel grooves; Knidian hairdressing (hair tied back into a bun, from the Aphrodite of Knidos) remained popular, small curls hung round the neck from c. 250 BC; most elaborate Hellenistic style: Heracles knot. — [Walters 23.140 (lampadion)](https://art.thewalters.org/object/23.140/); [Walters 48.290 (melon, c. 230 BC)](https://art.thewalters.org/object/48.290/); [world4.eu coiffures](https://world4.eu/ancient-greece-coiffures/)
- Korymbos: Athenian women's coil at the nape; girls simply coiled up their hair. — [world4.eu](https://world4.eu/ancient-greece-coiffures/)
- Met holds a gold-and-garnet strap diadem with Heracles knot (3rd-2nd c. BC), gold armband with Heracles knot with garnets, emeralds, enamel, and a massive solid-gold snake bracelet (Ptolemaic, early Hellenistic). — [Met 250990](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/250990); [Met 256970](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/256970); [Met 255898](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/255898)
- Heracles knot: popular motif, thought to cure wounds, marriage symbol. — [Daily Art Magazine](https://www.dailyartmagazine.com/jewelry-in-ancient-greece/)
- Hellenistic types: earrings, necklaces, pendants, pins, bracelets, armbands, thigh bands, rings, wreaths, diadems. — [Met essay: Hellenistic Jewelry](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/hellenistic-jewelry) (via search snippet)
- Jewellery mostly gold/silver, with pearls, gems; passed down generations. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)

### Inferences
- Hellenistic gold is polychrome (garnet, carnelian, enamel, pearls, emerald) unlike Classical plain gold; red garnet is the signature accent [BG].

### Gaps
- Earring types (disc, boat, Eros-pendant) not sourced; Met pages 429-limited.

## Textiles, dyes, class, special dress

### Takeaway
Wool and linen dominate; silk (Kos) and Amorgos fine fabric are luxury; purple is prestige; wide palette; border and embroidery for decoration.

### Cited Findings
- Wool preferred for warmth/dye, linen early; silk ceremonial for the wealthy; sindon fine light summer cloth; palette black, red, yellow, blue, green, purple; embroidered florals, geometrics, myth scenes; yellow linked with women's life cycle. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)
- Amorgos robes prized, nearly transparent, very costly. — [Wikipedia: Greek dress (search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_dress)
- Purple (Tyrian) from Phoenicians, copied by Greek cities. — [Tyrian purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrian_purple)
- Mourning: grey or black garments; nearest relations cut hair; corpse laid out in white chiton and himation. — [Burial Customs of the Ancient Greeks](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/49431/49431-h/49431-h.htm)
- Persian trousers replaced ancestral dress in Tomis only by early 1st c. AD (Ovid) - Roman-period, avoid for Aegean Greeks. — [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_clothing)

### Inferences
- Realism rule: ordinary people in undyed/brown/grey/ochre wool; bright dyes and purple for rich; "white marble" colourless look is a modern error.
- Anachronisms to avoid: Roman toga, tunica with clavi, paenula, calcei, Roman-style hairstyle (Flavian curls, 1st c. AD+), Persian trousers on Greeks, plate "Greek key" everywhere, bare-chested and sleeveless Hollywood chitons, white-only palette, bound-sandal "gladiator" (caligae) lace-up, laurel wreaths for everyone [BG].

### Gaps
- Not covered by sources: pins/fibulae types, weights (gonu-weights sewn into hem [BG]), wreath types by occasion (myrtle, ivy for Dionysus, olive, gold wreaths of the Hellenistic elite) beyond jewellery, priests'/magistrates' dress (white, purple-bordered, fillets [BG]), children's dress (bullae are Roman; Greek children in short chiton/ himation, hair in tufts/ponytails [BG]), fishermen/sailors beyond exomis, regional variation (Macedonian kausia/chlamys, Egyptian-Greek influence in Alexandria, Doric peplos persistence in Sparta/Crete) beyond what is above. Heralds' kit beyond Hermes iconography not sourced.
