## 1. Coinage, weights and measures

### Takeaway
The Attic reckoning (6 obols = 1 drachma, 100 drachmas = 1 mina, 60 minas = 1 talent) is the working frame. Alexander-type, Ptolemaic, Seleucid, Rhodian and Athenian coin types supply the visuals. I found no sourced chalkous equivalence and no sourced civic types for small island poleis.

### Cited Findings
- Attic standard figures: talent = 60 minae = 25.86 kg; mina = 100 drachmae = 431 g; drachma = 6 obols = 4.31 g; obol = 0.72 g silver. (Aeginetic: talent 37.8 kg, mina 630 g, drachma 6.3 g.) — [Wikipedia, Ancient Greek units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_units_of_measurement)
- Attic silver denominations: decadrachm 10 dr (~43 g), tetradrachm 4 dr (~17.2 g), didrachm 2 dr (~8.6 g), drachma 6 ob (~4.3 g), obol (~0.72 g; = 4 tetartemoria). — [Wikipedia, Ancient Greek coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_coinage)
- Hellenistic novelty: portraits of living people, namely the kings, on coins (Ptolemaic, Seleucid, Indo-Greek). — [Wikipedia, Ancient Greek coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_coinage)
- Volume: medimnos = 48 choinikes = 52.4 L; choinix = 24 kyathoi = 1.09 L ("daily grain ration of an adult man"); metretes = 12 choes = 39.3 L (amphora unit); kotyle varied 210-330 ml by place. — [Wikipedia, units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_units_of_measurement)
- Length: stadion = 600 podes = 184.9 m (Attic-based); plethron = 100 podes = 30.8 m; pous = 16 daktyloi = 308.2 mm; orguia (fathom) = 6 podes = 1.85 m. — same source. Note the project rule "no dimensions" applies to places; these are for reference, not for prose.
- Rhodian standard: early tetradrachm 15.6 g, didrachm 7.8 g, later lighter (minimum 6.7 g for didrachms); Helios head and rose (a pun on the island's name); ship's prow also used. After the siege of 305-304 BC Rhodian didrachms rose in eastern trade; the system ended c. 30 BC. By c. 300 BC the Aegean was dominated by Attic and Rhodian standards. — [Wikipedia, Rhodian coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodian_coinage)
- Delos accounts: the hieropoioi published annual accounts on stone for about 145 years (314-167 BC); they recorded totals, not unit prices or per-day wages, though the data reflect ordinary market prices. Reger (1994) argues local and regional supply, not Mediterranean-wide conditions, drove price movements. (search summary) — [search result via Kron/Scheidel-type pages](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/a-few-ports/delos/); see the Gaps for the missing primary check.
- Grain: wheat normally 5-6 dr per medimnos and barley 3-5 dr in a 3rd c. BC summary; 257 BC (probably Ptolemaic Egypt) a worker earned 1 obol a day, buying 2 choinikes of wheat. (search summary) — [Coinweek, Prices and coins](https://coinweek.com/prices-and-coins-in-the-ancient-world/)
- Delos rents: sacred leases of Apollo's estates ran from 21 dr (Limne) to 1,113 dr (Chareteia) a year, most 250-1,000 dr. — [Attalus, Delos hiera syngraphe](https://www.attalus.org/docs/other/inscr_223.html)
- Stele cost, 3rd c. Delos, about 135 dr total (stone 25 dr, engraving 100 dr at 1 dr per 300 letters). Carried over from pass 1; primary text still unverified. — [Bonanno 2017](https://classicsmalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Bonanno-2017-A-Friend-in-High-Places.pdf)
- Commons coin images with licences: see section 9.

### Inferences
- A mid-size city's pay or fine in drachmas is best given as multiples of a labourer's day (1-2 dr in 4th c. Attica, see pass 1). A stele at ~135 dr is on the order of 70-130 labour days. [Inference.]
- Small island poleis most likely issued small bronze for local use and bought silver in Alexander-type or Rhodian coin. [Inference; no sourced island type list.]

### Gaps
- Chalkous rate (conventionally 1/8 obol): not found in a fetched source. Do not state it without verifying.
- Civic coin types for Tenos, Paros, Naxos, Amorgos, Kos etc.: not found. Next route: ANS MANTIS (numismatics.org) and Hellenistic Greek Coin catalogues.
- Athenian New Style coinage: Wikipedia page 404; no sourced description (c. 165 BC, wreathed owl, magistrate names are from background knowledge only).
- Primary Delos price figures (IG XI 2 accounts) not retrieved; Reger 1994 only via summary.
- Interest on maritime loans and the Delos bankers: no sourced Hellenistic maritime-loan figures.

## 2. Banking, interest, harbour dues, customs, tax farming, grain

### Takeaway
Sanctuaries were the cities' banks. Delos' Apollo lent at 10% a year on mortgaged property. Harbour dues were farmed out to the highest bidder, and Polybius gives the best hard number: Rhodes' harbour dues were farmed at 1,000,000 dr a year, then 150,000 after Delos became a free port.

### Cited Findings
- Polybius 30.31.4 (Rhodian envoys at Rome): harbour dues "in former times were farmed for a million drachmae, they now fetch only a hundred and fifty thousand." Same chapter: Caunus and Stratonicea yielded 120 talents a year; Rhodes had bought Caunus from Ptolemy's generals for 200 talents and had to give it up on Roman orders. — [Polybius 30 at Perseus/Thayer](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/30*.html)
- Delos was made a free port in 167/166 BC; Rhodes' dues fell as a result. — [Wikipedia, Piracy in the ancient Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_ancient_Mediterranean); [Wikipedia, Delos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delos)
- Strabo (as cited): up to ten thousand slaves could be sold on Delos in one day. — [Wikipedia, Piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_ancient_Mediterranean)
- Apollo's treasury on Delos lent at 10% a year (5th-2nd c. BC); houses served as collateral. (search summary) — [CAMWS abstract, Do ut debt](http://forms.camws.org/Abstracts2017/250.DoUtDebt.pdf); [Cotrugli, Banking in ancient Greece](https://cotrugli.org/banking-in-ancient-greece/)
- Trapezitai: bankers named after the "table" (trapeza); took deposits, lent, changed coin. (search summary) — [Wikipedia, Banker (ancient)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banker_(ancient))
- Delos hiera syngraphe (leases of the god's land, 300 BC and 250 BC): ten-year leases; each lessee needed two guarantors liable together; all the lessee's goods, animals, slaves and furniture "are to be subject to the god"; late payment carried a hemiolion (+50%); defaulters were posted on stone with their fathers' names. A guarantor who paid became creditor for one and a half times the sum. — [Attalus, inscr. 223](https://www.attalus.org/docs/other/inscr_223.html)
- Athens' 2% (pentekoste) on trade into the Piraeus was levied in 404/3 BC and yielded 36 talents; an inscription from Delos gives pentekoste revenue, so the tax was collected there too; duties were let by auction to tax-farmers. (search summary) — [Kron 2015, Greek trade](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/AUTHORS/Kron2015-GreekTrade.pdf); [Athenian Grain-Tax Law, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_Grain-Tax_Law_of_374/3_B.C.)
- Samos grain law (Syll.3 976, c. 260 BC): a public grain fund; one curator elected per chiliastys lent the money out; the interest went to a board of two, who handed it to the elected grain buyer; Samos taxed grain grown at Anaia to support it. The text shows civic finance leaning on rich citizens. (search summary) — [Attalus, SIG index](https://www.attalus.org/docs/sig.html); [Bagnall reading](https://www.columbia.edu/itc/classics/bagnall/3995/readings/b-d2-3.htm)
- Athenian sitonai honoured in Hellenistic decrees: IG II3 1 908 and 1017 (Attic Inscriptions Online). — [AIO 908](https://www.atticinscriptions.com/inscription/IGII31/908); [AIO 1017](https://www.atticinscriptions.com/inscription/IGII31/1017)

### Inferences
- A 10% rate is the sanctuary's rate; private rates and maritime loans (higher, risk-priced) are not sourced here. A modest island sanctuary probably lent similarly. [Inference.]
- The 150,000 / 1,000,000 ratio suggests that, at a 2% rate, trade of about 50 million dr fell to about 7.5 million (a figure the search summary computed; the 2% rate for Rhodes itself is not sourced, so treat that arithmetic as illustrative only).

### Gaps
- No source for a 2% rate specifically at Rhodes. Do not assert it.
- Maritime loan rates, trapezitai at Delos specifically (bankers' accounts), the Delian bank of Apollo's deposit practice: not verified at primary level.
- Rhodes' "1 million" is a lease price of the tax, not a count of trade; keep it as farmed revenue.

## 3. Decrees, assemblies, courts, archives

### Takeaway
A decree was moved by a named proposer, voted by council and people, awarded honours, and then sent to be inscribed and set up in a sanctuary. The Theangela decree for Semos is a concrete, citable example of a ransom story with the whole publication chain.

### Cited Findings
- Decree formulae: "It seemed best to the council," "to the people," or "to the council and people." The council prepared the agenda (probouleuma) for the assembly. — [Stoa.org, The Council](http://www.stoa.org/demos/article_council@page=12&greekEncoding=UnicodeC.html) (search summary)
- Athenian prytany procedure: a prytany of one tribe ran the council for a tenth of the year; each day a chairman (epistates) was chosen by lot and held the state seal and the keys to the treasuries and archives; no one served twice. Rhodes, Alexandria and west-coast Asia Minor cities also had prytaneis presiding over councils. — [Wikipedia, Prytaneis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prytaneis)
- Theangela decree for Semos of Delos, IG XI 4 1054 (c. 230-220 BC): Semos bought women of Theangela out of pirates' booty, treated them as free women with honour; the assembly voted 620 votes in favour; honours were proxeny, citizenship with tribe, hereditary rights, inscription on the public stele of benefactors, and a memorial stone on Delos. Envoys Pausanias son of Theodotos and Thrason were chosen to carry the decree to Delos and have the stele placed. — [Attalus, IG XI 4 1054](http://www.attalus.org/docs/other/inscr_20.html)
- Doubt among scholars whether Semos acted for honour or for ransom money. (search summary) — [Pirates thesis, UNL](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1768&context=honorstheses)
- Foreign judges: attested often in the 3rd and above all the 2nd c. BC, called dikastai (occasionally diallaktai). Requesters: Delphi, Gonnoi, Iasos, Demetrias, Alabanda, Smyrna. Suppliers: Messene, Kos, Priene most often; also Thasos, Miletos, Iasos, Erythrai. Special elected officials (dikastagogoi) managed the visit. Decrees honouring them are formulaic. — [Scafuro, decrees for foreign judges](https://www.austriaca.at/0xc1aa5576_0x0031f1cb.pdf) (search summary); Kos reconciliation decree IG XII 4 1 132 — [Telos reconciliation decree](https://classicalstudies.org/writing-memorialization-and-stasis-reconciliation-decree-telos-ig-xii-4-1-132) (search summary)
- Royal letters were inscribed on stone by recipient cities. Letters for Kos (RC 21, 25-28, 50), Teos (RC 3-4, 35), Iasos (RC 49), Teos' Dionysiac artists (RC 53), Eumenes II to the Ionian League (RC 52). — [Attalus, Royal Correspondence index](https://www.attalus.org/docs/rc/index.html)
- Kleroterion and the allotment of jurors: bronze pinakia (identity tickets) and voting tokens survive in the Athens Agora Museum; see section 9 for pictures.
- Dodona oracle: over 4,000 lead tablets of questions, late 6th to mid 2nd c. BC; questions on marriage, children, travel, health, military service, buying slaves, manumission; replies mostly yes/no, probably by lot. — [Cambridge, Visiting the Oracle at Dodona](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/visiting-the-oracle-at-dodona/consultation-and-cognition/F023AFD77CEE6828C97F9659D83E366C); [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/lead-tablets-dodona-oracle) (search summary)

### Inferences
- A small-polis assembly decree likely followed this shape: a council motion, a vote counted in the hundreds (620 at Theangela is a sample of a fair-sized small city), then an elected envoy carrying a copy. [Inference.]
- Honorific stele text is a cheap proxy for what the herald proclaimed in the theatre or at the festival. [Inference.]

### Gaps
- Meeting place, seating, herald's opening prayer and sacrifice, ballot types and counting for a Hellenistic small island polis: not found. No fetched source for archeia, notaries, sealed contracts, witness procedure or oaths. Try PHI (epigraphy.packhum.org), IG XII 5 and 7 (Tenos, Paros, Amorgos) and Harland's association translations.
- How news and orders travelled to villages (heralds, tablets): not found.
- Water clocks used in courts: only the Agora Museum clepsydra photos (section 9); no sourced timing figures.

## 4. Rhodes, leagues, kingdoms, garrisons

### Takeaway
Rhodes was run by an assembly, a council that sat six months at a time, five prytaneis, and strategoi and nauarchs. Kings reached island cities through garrison commanders, league officials and royal letters.

### Cited Findings
- Rhodes: the assembly elected a council (boule) and 5 prytaneis serving 6 months each, who presided in the council; with strategoi and nauarchoi they formed the government. Councils sat for six months (winter or summer). — [Wikipedia-derived summary, search result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prytaneis) (search summary; the Prytaneis page itself only confirms Rhodes had prytaneis). Scholarship: Gabrielsen, The Naval Aristocracy of Hellenistic Rhodes — [JHS review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-hellenic-studies/article/abs/v-gabrielsen-the-naval-aristocracy-of-hellenistic-rhodes-studies-in-hellenistic-civilization-6-aarhus-oxford-and-oakville-aarhus-up-1997-pp-viii-239-2495-8772884306/F1610A8F182A7E13D7EC6E8C1D8F02A6)
- Rhodes led the League of the Islanders and used it against pirates. — [Wikipedia, Piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_ancient_Mediterranean)
- Aetolian League: strategos (commander and president), hipparch, grammateus, the three eponymous officials. The assembly met twice a year, at the Thermika (autumn) and the Panaitolika (spring), at Thermos. The council grew to about 1,500; a smaller apokletoi handled daily business. From c. 260 BC: seven tamiai and seven epilektarchoi, plus boularchoi. From 278 the league sent delegates to the Amphictyony and eventually had a majority. — [Wikipedia, Aetolian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetolian_League)
- Phrourarch = garrison commander. Ptolemaic admiral Patroklos set up bases at Hydrea, Thera and Itanos (Crete), and Keos, Attica and the Argolid; at Thera in 267/6 BC he left Apollodotos as base commander, and the commander governed the island. The Thera garrison lasted into Ptolemy VI's reign. (search summary) — [Wikipedia, Phrourarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrourarch); [Wikipedia, Patroclus (admiral)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patroclus_(admiral)); [Ptolemy I and II's forces](https://warhistory.org/article/ptolemy-i-iis-military-forces)
- Nesiotic League, nesiarch, Delos synedrion, tamias and borrowing from Apollo: see pass 1 section 2.
- Artists of Dionysus: guilds with city-like organisation (priests, treasurers, secretaries, archons); a Delphi decree of 279-277 BC granted asylia, safe travel, exemption from military service and tax exemption; bases at Athens, Argos, Thebes, Teos. — [Wikipedia, Artists of Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_of_Dionysus)
- Delos temple records: hieropoioi (annual) published accounts and inventories of dedications on stone; about a fifth of the Delos inscriptions are such accounts. (search summary) — [Hesperia, Temple estates of Delos](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/146922.pdf)
- Sarapis sanctuary C inventory, Delos, 155/154 BC: gold crowns and silver vessels in the Artemis temple, items in sanctuaries of Isis, Anubis and Sarapis; the "koinon of therapeutai" and "Sarapiastai gathered by Menneas" appear with named priests. — [Harland, Associations](https://philipharland.com/greco-roman-associations/inventories-of-the-sarapis-sanctuary-mentioning-therapeutists-and-sarapiasts-155154-bce/)
- Piracy: Cilicia and Illyria were endemic zones; Illyrian lembus; Queen Teuta issued letters of marque; Delos was a notorious slave market. — [Wikipedia, Piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_ancient_Mediterranean)
- Gymnasium: after Macedonian power, one gymnasiarch was chosen annually and ran order and discipline for the ephebes, sometimes paying heavy costs himself; he organised religious festivals three times a year, the chief for Hermes. Athens ran a satellite ephebeia on Delos after 166 BC. (search summary) — [Wikipedia, Gymnasiarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasiarch); [EFA, Delos history](https://www.efa.gr/histoire-de-delos-3/?lang=en)
- Writing materials: ostraca, wax tablets (wooden frame, beeswax, stylus), wood, lead, linen, occasionally leather; reed pen and papyrus. Tablets used for everyday bookkeeping. (search summary) — [Palaeography ch. 2](http://www.katapi.org.uk/G&LPalaeography/Ch2.html)

### Inferences
- Antigonid/Ptolemaic garrison commander on an island was both soldier and regional governor, so he is the visible king's man to villagers. [Inference from the Thera case.]
- A decree for a ransomer plus a pirate-slave market on Delos implies that a ransom ledger and honours were a repeating civic routine. [Inference.]

### Gaps
- Attalid administration, Antigonid administration (Wikipedia Antigonid page had nothing), Rhodian nauarch/strategos duties and Rhodian maritime law: not sourced.
- Ransom prices: none found.
- Slavery and manumission on island poleis: only Delphi (pass 1). Festival calendars: none.
- Literacy rates: none. Ephebic reforms specific to small island cities: none.
